# Nutrition: practical lessons adults wish school had taught (SA first, then UK, US, global)

Research date: 4 November 2026. 15 search/fetch calls. Each claim below cites a source fetched or returned in search this session. Items flagged **[unverified this session]** come from background knowledge only and must be checked before they go into a lesson. They sit in the Gaps sections.

**Proposed lesson priority, worked out from the findings below:**
1. What a healthy plate looks like (SA food guide, then Eatwell, MyPlate/new pyramid, WHO). Core numbers: salt, sugar, fat, fibre, fruit and veg, water.
2. Reading a food label (SA R146 now, with R3337 front-of-pack warnings pending; UK traffic lights; US Nutrition Facts).
3. Food safety at home (fridge temperature, the 2-hour rule, leftovers, cross-contamination). SA hook: the 2017–18 listeriosis outbreak traced to polony.
4. Sugar and salt: why governments tax and regulate them (SA Health Promotion Levy, SA mandatory sodium limits).
5. Eating well on a tight budget, plus basic cooking confidence.
6. Myths, fad diets, supplements, protein hype, and the limits of BMI.
7. Babies and children: breastfeeding, first foods, no added sugar or salt.

## Official healthy-eating guides: what SA, UK, US and WHO say (portions, salt, sugar, fats, fibre, water)

### Takeaway
All four agree on the core messages: mostly whole or minimally processed foods, plenty of fruit, veg, pulses and whole grains, and limits on salt, sugar and saturated fat. SA's guide is unusual because it pictures only foods to eat and leaves out "limit" foods. The US 2025–2030 guidelines (Jan 2026) differ most: a stricter line on added sugar, higher protein targets, and an inverted "food pyramid" that replaces MyPlate's prominence. Scientists have criticised the mixed messages on saturated fat.

### Cited Findings
**South Africa (FBDGs)**
- SA first published FBDGs in 2003 and launched a revised version in 2012. They were developed by the Nutrition Society of SA with the Department of Health, the MRC, academics and UN agencies, and adopted by the NDoH Directorate of Nutrition. The general FBDGs target people aged 5 and older; separate paediatric FBDGs cover ages 0–5. — [FAO FBDG – South Africa](https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/regions/countries/south-africa/en/)
- The 11 SA messages: Enjoy a variety of foods. Be active! Make starchy foods part of most meals. Eat plenty of vegetables and fruit every day. Eat dry beans, split peas, lentils and soya regularly. Have milk, maas or yoghurt every day. Fish, chicken, lean meat or eggs can be eaten daily. Drink lots of clean, safe water. Use fats sparingly; choose vegetable oils rather than hard fats. Use sugar and foods and drinks high in sugar sparingly. Use salt and food high in salt sparingly. — [FAO FBDG – South Africa](https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/regions/countries/south-africa/en/)
- The SA food guide shows 7 groups: starchy foods; vegetables and fruit; dry beans, peas, lentils and soya; chicken, fish, meat and eggs; milk, maas, yoghurt; fat and oil; and water. Each segment's size reflects its share of the daily diet. Unlike other guides, it leaves out foods to limit (highly processed foods high in sugar, fat and salt). — [FAO FBDG – South Africa](https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/regions/countries/south-africa/en/)
- SA also has separate FBDGs for older people. — [Tandfonline: FBDGs for the Elderly in SA](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/16070658.2021.1950376)
- A University of Pretoria narrative review notes that recently updated FBDGs internationally emphasise sustainability, cultural relevance, digital and visual tools, and ultra-processed foods. — [UP repository review](https://repository.up.ac.za/bitstreams/13960e01-8b29-417a-8bbf-32ba115b4b83/download)

**United Kingdom (Eatwell Guide / NHS)**
- Adults should have no more than 30 g of free sugars a day, roughly 7 sugar cubes. The front-of-pack reference intake for total sugars is 90 g a day, which includes 30 g free sugars. — [NHS: Sugar, the facts](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/food-types/how-does-sugar-in-our-diet-affect-our-health/)
- Eatwell: aim for 6–8 glasses of fluid a day; check labels and avoid foods high in fat, salt and sugar. — [GOV.WALES Eatwell guide](https://www.gov.wales/eatwell-guide-html)
- 6–8 glasses of sugar-free fluid such as water, milk, unsweetened tea or coffee. — [Diabetes UK on Eatwell](https://www.diabetes.org.uk/living-with-diabetes/eating/the-eatwell-guide)
- NHS inform Scotland's Eatwell page (Public Health Scotland) was updated 14 Oct 2026. — [NHS inform Eatwell](https://www.nhsinform.scot/healthy-living/food-and-nutrition/eating-well/eatwell-guide-how-to-eat-a-healthy-balanced-diet/)

**United States (Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2025–2030)**
- Released in January 2026 under the tagline "eat real food". For the first time it names "highly processed foods" as a category to limit. It is accompanied by an inverted pyramid-style graphic that replaces MyPlate's prominence. — [Harvard Nutrition Source, 9 Jan 2026](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/2026/01/09/dietary-guidelines-for-americans-2025-2030/); full text at [cdn.realfood.gov/DGA.pdf](https://cdn.realfood.gov/DGA.pdf); hub [DietaryGuidelines.gov](https://www.dietaryguidelines.gov/)
- Added sugar: "no amount of added sugars or non-nutritive sweeteners is recommended". No single meal should contain more than 10 g of added sugar. Children should avoid added sugar until age 10 (previously age 2). The previous limit was 10% of calories (about 50 g/day). — [Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/2026/01/09/dietary-guidelines-for-americans-2025-2030/); [CACFP summary](https://www.cacfp.org/2026/01/08/2025-2030-dietary-guidelines-for-americans-released/)
- Snack sugar limits follow FDA "Healthy" claim limits, e.g. grain snacks no more than 5 g added sugar per ¾ oz whole-grain equivalent, and yoghurt no more than 2.5 g per ⅔ cup. — [CACFP](https://www.cacfp.org/2026/01/08/2025-2030-dietary-guidelines-for-americans-released/)
- Saturated fat stays below 10% of calories, but the "healthy fats" guidance groups butter, beef tallow and full-fat dairy with plant oils. Harvard's worked example: 3 full-fat dairy servings plus 1 tbsp butter already exceeds about 22 g, the sat-fat limit on 2,000 kcal. — [Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/2026/01/09/dietary-guidelines-for-americans-2025-2030/)
- Protein: 1.2–1.6 g/kg body weight/day, which is 50–100% above the previous minimum. Harvard calls this "protein hype". — [Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/2026/01/09/dietary-guidelines-for-americans-2025-2030/)
- Whole grains: 2–4 servings/day. Alcohol: only "consume less", with no numeric limit. — [Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/2026/01/09/dietary-guidelines-for-americans-2025-2030/)
- Process controversy: the independent advisory committee report was rejected and replaced by a contracted "supplemental scientific analysis". Reviewers' ties to the beef and dairy industries were disclosed. — [Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/2026/01/09/dietary-guidelines-for-americans-2025-2030/)
- Stanford: there is broad consensus on limiting added sugar, sodium and highly processed foods, but the protein targets are hard to meet without exceeding the sat-fat and sodium limits. — [Stanford Medicine Nutrition](https://med.stanford.edu/nutrition/news/press/2025_2030_Dietary_Guidelines.html)
- Companion documents: Daily Servings by Calorie Level. — [cdn.realfood.gov/Daily Serving Sizes.pdf](https://cdn.realfood.gov/Daily%20Serving%20Sizes.pdf)

**WHO (global), fact sheet updated 26 Jan 2026**
- Four principles: adequacy, balance, moderation, diversity. A diet must also be safe, i.e. free from microbial and chemical contaminants. — [WHO Healthy diet fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- Fruit and veg: at least 400 g/day for those over 10 (250 g for ages 2–5, 350 g for ages 6–9). Fibre: at least 25 g/day naturally occurring (15 g for ages 2–5, 21 g for ages 6–9). Carbohydrates: about 45–75% of energy, mainly from whole grains, veg, fruit and pulses. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- Free sugars: below 10% of energy, about 50 g or 12 level teaspoons on 2,000 kcal; 5% or less gives further benefit. Free sugars include honey, syrups and fruit juice. Sugar should be cut **without** using non-sugar sweeteners. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- Fat: 15–30% of energy for adults; saturated fat at most 10%; trans fat at most 1%; avoid industrial trans fat. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- Protein: 10–15% of energy, about 50–75 g/day on 2,000 kcal, is generally enough. Excess protein can burden the kidneys. This contrasts with the US 1.2–1.6 g/kg. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- Salt: below 5 g/day (2 g sodium) for adults. Potassium: at least 3,510 mg/day. Most salt comes from processed food and bread. WHO recommends lower-sodium salt substitutes, except for people at risk of high potassium. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- WHO explicitly recommends "educating children, adolescents and adults about nutrition" and "encouraging culinary skills, including in children through schools". — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)

### Inferences
- Comparing salt guidance across countries makes a good teaching moment: WHO says below 5 g; SA's FBDG says only "sparingly". UK and US numbers were not verified this session (see Gaps).
- The US guidance is currently contested. The course should present it as "the US government says X; major academic nutrition departments disagree on Y" rather than treating it as settled. WHO numbers are the safest default for the global course.
- SA's FBDGs date from 2012 and may be under revision. Check for a newer edition before publishing.

### Gaps
- **SA FBDG revision:** there are hints of an update (a Facebook post about "updating SA FBDGs"), but I found no official revised edition. Check with NSSA / NDoH. Primary PDF not fetched; the 2013 SAJCN supplement (S Afr J Clin Nutr 26(3) Suppl) is the usual citation **[unverified this session]**.
- **UK numbers not fetched from the primary source:** adult salt max 6 g/day, fibre 30 g/day, at least 5 portions of fruit and veg of 80 g each. Eatwell is published by OHID/PHE under the Open Government Licence v3.0 **[unverified this session]**. Fetch [gov.uk Eatwell Guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-eatwell-guide).
- **US sodium figure** (traditionally under 2,300 mg/day) and the status of the MyPlate site were not confirmed for 2025–2030.
- **SA portion sizes:** no official SA portion table found.

## Reading food labels (SA R146 / R3337 front-of-pack, UK traffic lights, US Nutrition Facts)

### Takeaway
SA: R146 (2010) is still the law in force. The Chile-style front-of-pack (FOP) warning labels in draft R3337 (warnings for high sugar, sodium and saturated fat, plus a separate warning for artificial sweeteners) are **still not final as of Oct–Nov 2026**. Civil society is pushing for approval by 10 Dec 2026. US: the FDA's front-of-package "Nutrition Info box" is still a proposed rule, not final. UK: voluntary traffic lights (details not fetched).

### Cited Findings
- "R3337 remains a draft as of mid-2026 and is not yet in force. R146 of 2010 is still the labelling law you must comply with today." — [ASC Food Safety (SA consultancy), Jul 2026 (updated Oct 2026)](https://ascfoodsafety.com/front-of-pack-and-regulatory-defence/)
- Draft R3337 sets mandatory FOP warnings on the principal display panel for high sugar, sodium or saturated fat, and a distinct warning where non-nutritive sweeteners are present. One product can carry several warnings. It adapts the Chilean model in a **triangular** format. Products carrying warnings would face restrictions on child-directed marketing (cartoon characters, toys, competitions) and lose access to many claims. — [ASC Food Safety](https://ascfoodsafety.com/front-of-pack-and-regulatory-defence/)
- Draft saturated-fat thresholds: at least 4 g/100 g for solids and at least 3 g/100 ml for liquids. R3337 was published for comment in 2023. — [USDA FAS GAIN report SF2023-0020](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/newgainapi/api/Report/DownloadReportByFileName?fileName=South%20Africa%20Issues%20New%20Draft%20Regulation%20on%20Food%20Labelling_Pretoria_South%20Africa%20-%20Republic%20of_SF2023-0020.pdf)
- Health-e News op-ed (8 Oct 2026): a public countdown calls on the Minister of Health to approve R3337 by 10 December 2026. — [Health-e News](https://health-e.org.za/2026/10/08/op-ed-south-africans-have-waited-long-enough-for-front-of-pack-warning-labels/); [HEALA](https://heala.org/op-ed-south-africans-have-waited-long-enough-for-front-of-pack-warning-labels/)
- A peer-reviewed study modelling R3337 thresholds against SA porridges exists. — [Tandfonline 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/16070658.2025.2603783)
- US: the FDA proposed rule (16 Jan 2025) would require a compact "Nutrition Info box" on the front of most packaged foods that carry a Nutrition Facts label, with compliance 3 years after the final rule (for firms with $10M+ in sales). — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/16/2025-00778/food-labeling-front-of-package-nutrition-information); [FDA FOP page](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/front-package-nutrition-labeling)
- The FDA projected a final rule for May 2026, then said it would spend 2026 summarising comments and preparing options. As of early 2026 it was not finalised. — [Packaging Digest](https://www.packagingdigest.com/regulations-standards/fda-front-of-pack-labeling-rule-2026-status-and-timeline); [FoodLabelMaker](https://foodlabelmaker.com/regulatory-hub/fda/front-of-pack-labeling/)
- UK FOP reference intake for total sugars is 90 g/day. — [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/food-types/how-does-sugar-in-our-diet-affect-our-health/)
- WHO recommends interpretive FOP labelling and Codex-aligned nutrition labels. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)

### Inferences
- The SA lesson should teach reading today's R146 back-of-pack nutrition table (per 100 g lets you compare products) and preview the warning triangles as "coming soon". Build it so the content can be switched over once R3337 is gazetted.
- Teach the "per 100 g" comparison as a universal skill that works across all three countries.
- The sweetener warning teaches a useful lesson: "diet" does not mean healthy. This lines up with WHO's advice against non-sugar sweeteners.

### Gaps
- R3337's exact sugar and sodium thresholds were not retrieved; only the saturated-fat thresholds were confirmed.
- UK traffic-light thresholds (e.g. per 100 g: sugar over 22.5 g red, at most 5 g green; salt over 1.5 g red, at most 0.3 g green) **[unverified this session]**. Fetch from the [FSA/DHSC FOP guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/front-of-pack-nutrition-labelling-guidance). The UK's HFSS advertising restrictions (in force Jan 2026?) are also unverified.
- US Nutrition Facts panel basics (%DV, "added sugars" line, the 5%/20% rule of thumb) not fetched. Source: [FDA "How to Understand and Use the Nutrition Facts Label"](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/how-understand-and-use-nutrition-facts-label).
- Date-marking (best before vs use by): not researched.

## Food safety at home (storage, fridge temperatures, cross-contamination, leftovers; SA listeriosis lessons)

### Takeaway
Universal rules: keep the fridge at or below 5 °C / 40 °F, chill leftovers within 2 hours (1 hour in heat over 32 °C / 90 °F), and treat ready-to-eat processed meats as a listeria risk for pregnant women, babies, older people and anyone immunocompromised. SA had the world's largest and deadliest listeriosis outbreak in 2017–18, from polony. That makes a strong, locally relevant hook.

### Cited Findings
- USDA FSIS: discard cold leftovers left out more than 2 hours at room temperature, or 1 hour above 90 °F. — [USDA FSIS Leftovers and Food Safety](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- FDA consumer guidance on storage: perishables at 45 °F or below (thermometer-checked) after a power cut should be safe but should be cooked and eaten soon. — [FDA: Are You Storing Food Safely?](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/are-you-storing-food-safely)
- UK best practice is to keep the fridge below 5 °C. — [High Speed Training (UK trainer, secondary)](https://www.highspeedtraining.co.uk/hub/safe-temperatures-for-food-storage/)
- SA outbreak, 2017–18: described as the world's largest listeriosis outbreak; cases rose from mid-June 2017. — [PMC: Outbreak of L. monocytogenes in SA 2017–2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6653791/)
- NDoH July 2018 report: 1,060 lab-confirmed cases and 216 deaths. Before 2017, SA confirmed 60–80 cases a year. Traced to the Enterprise Foods (Tiger Brands) processed-meat factory in Polokwane, announced 4 March 2018. Polony was the main vehicle, found via samples at a crèche. — [Wikipedia, citing the NDoH 2018 report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%932018_South_African_listeriosis_outbreak)
- WHO at 28 March 2018: outcome known for 674 patients, 183 (27%) of whom died. — [WHO Disease Outbreak News](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/28-march-2018-listeriosis-south-africa-en)
- IAFP "lessons learned" webinar figure: 1,033 cases and 204 deaths at that point. — [IAFP webinar PDF](https://www.foodprotection.org/members/files/6_7_18_Webinar.pdf)
- WHO: a healthy diet must be safe, i.e. free from microbial and chemical contaminants. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)

### Inferences
- Case and death counts differ by report date (1,033/204 vs 1,060/216). Cite the final NDoH figure, 1,060/216, and link the primary NDoH or NICD report.
- The SA lesson can tie listeria to the "vulnerable groups" message: pregnant women should avoid ready-to-eat cold meats unless reheated until steaming. The FSA/NHS say the same.

### Gaps
- Primary FSA pages (fridge at or below 5 °C, eat leftovers within 2 days, cool within 90 minutes, reheat once until steaming hot, the "4 Cs") were not fetched. **[unverified this session]**: fetch [food.gov.uk](https://www.food.gov.uk/safety-hygiene).
- The SA government's own consumer food-safety guidance (NDoH / NICD) and the WHO "Five Keys to Safer Food" were not fetched. The WHO Five Keys would suit the global course.
- Load-shedding/power-cut food safety in SA: no SA official source found. FDA guidance on the 45 °F threshold above could adapt.
- What changed in SA law after the outbreak (e.g. listeriosis made a notifiable condition, R638 enforcement): not researched.

## Sugar tax (SA Health Promotion Levy) and SA salt reduction regulations

### Takeaway
SA's Health Promotion Levy (in force since April 2018) charges 2.1 c per gram of sugar above 4 g/100 ml. Within two years it cut sugar bought through taxable drinks by 33%, but the 2025 budget froze it, and advocates want it raised to the WHO-recommended 20%. SA was also among the first countries with *mandatory* sodium limits (2016, tightened 2019), and population salt intake fell by about 1.2 g/day.

### Cited Findings
- HPL: fixed at 2.1 cents per gram of sugar above 4 g/100 ml; the first 4 g/100 ml is levy-free. — [SARS: Health Promotion Levy on Sugary Beverages](https://www.sars.gov.za/customs-and-excise/excise/health-promotion-levy-on-sugary-beverages/)
- "Sugar" includes intrinsic and added sugars; introduced 2018. — [BeverageDaily, Apr 2018](https://www.beveragedaily.com/Article/2018/04/03/South-Africa-introduces-sugar-tax/)
- In the 2025 budget the finance minister announced no further increase; the levy was unchanged in 2025. — [Baijnath et al., Critical Public Health 2026 (search snippet)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09581596.2026.2687198)
- Within two years the levy caused a 33% reduction in sugar consumed through taxable drinks. — [UNU-WIDER, "Shifting sweetness"](https://www.wider.unu.edu/publication/shifting-sweetness-impacts-south-africas-health-promotion-levy-sugar-sweetened)
- A 2025 study found consumption of non-taxable sugary drinks rose 15%. — [HEALA (advocacy; secondary)](https://heala.org/budget-2026-a-chance-to-boost-sugary-drinks-tax-cut-costs-and-save-lives/)
- The HPL is below the WHO-recommended 20% tax level; a study estimates the cost of not raising it. — [PMC13358265](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13358265/)
- Effect on prices and purchases. — [PMC9991735](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9991735/)
- WHO recommends taxes on foods high in saturated fat, trans fat, free sugars and salt. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- Salt: SA's "Regulations relating to the reduction of sodium in certain foodstuffs" set first maximum levels from 30 June 2016, with a further reduction from 30 June 2019. — [USDA FAS GAIN report, Jul 2016](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/newgainapi/api/report/downloadreportbyfilename?filename=Regulations%20relating%20to%20the%20reduction%20of%20sodium%20in%20certain%20foodstuff_Pretoria_South%20Africa%20-%20Republic%20of_7-26-2016.pdf)
- 98% of bread samples complied, but more than 30% of products in bread, gravy powder, savoury sauce powder and uncured processed meat exceeded the targets. — [J. Food Composition & Analysis 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0889157523007822)
- Population salt intake fell about 1.16–1.2 g/day between 2015 and 2018/19. — [PMC10641639](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10641639/); [Action on Salt, African-PREDICT](https://www.actiononsalt.org.uk/news/news/2022-/2022-news-section/early-evidence-for-the-effectiveness-of-south-africas-legislation-on-salt-restriction-in-foods-the-african-predict-study.html)

### Inferences
- Lesson framing: "Why does Coke Zero cost less than Coke in SA?" makes taxes tangible. A "4 g/100 ml free" rule means a 330 ml can with about 35 g sugar pays levy on roughly 22 g. (This arithmetic is my own illustration; check against an actual can label.)
- Whether the 2026 budget changed the HPL was not confirmed. The search found advocacy calling for an increase but no confirmation either way.

### Gaps
- 2026 budget outcome for the HPL: not confirmed.
- Exact sodium targets per category (e.g. bread mg/100 g): not retrieved. Regulation number (R214 of 2013) **[unverified this session]**.
- UK Soft Drinks Industry Levy (2018; tiers at 5 g and 8 g/100 ml) and its 2025 changes **[unverified this session]**. The US has no federal soda tax, only city taxes **[unverified]**.

## Myths, fad diets, supplements, healthy weight/BMI and its limits; children's nutrition and breastfeeding

### Takeaway
Only partly researched. WHO gives authoritative infant-feeding rules: exclusive breastfeeding to 6 months, continued to 2 years and beyond, and no added salt or sugar in complementary foods. The protein disagreement (WHO 10–15% of energy vs US 1.2–1.6 g/kg) is a ready-made "myth-busting" topic. BMI, supplements and fad diets need follow-up sources.

### Cited Findings
- Infants: exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months; continue to 2 years and beyond; complementary foods from 6 months, with no added salt or sugar; children aged 6–23 months should have diverse diets including animal-source foods; avoid sweet drinks and foods high in sugar, salt and trans fat. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- US now advises no added sugar for children under 10. — [Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/2026/01/09/dietary-guidelines-for-americans-2025-2030/)
- SA has separate paediatric FBDGs for ages 0–5. — [FAO SA](https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/regions/countries/south-africa/en/)
- Myth fodder: "diet/zero" drinks are not a health fix. WHO advises against non-sugar sweeteners for weight control and sugar reduction, and SA's draft R3337 would put a warning on them. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet); [ASC](https://ascfoodsafety.com/front-of-pack-and-regulatory-defence/)
- Myth fodder: "more protein is always better". WHO says 10–15% of energy is generally enough and warns of a kidney burden from excess. Harvard says many Americans already eat more than enough protein. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet); [Harvard](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/2026/01/09/dietary-guidelines-for-americans-2025-2030/)
- Myth fodder: "fruit juice is as good as fruit". WHO counts juice sugars as free sugars. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- Myth fodder: "fresh is always better than frozen or tinned". WHO says frozen and canned are good if they have no added sugar or excess sodium. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- Low-carb: WHO says very low-carb diets are used to treat some conditions, but for most people unrefined carbs should be 45–75% of energy. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)

### Inferences
- Myth-busting works best as "what the evidence says vs the claim", using WHO numbers as the referee.

### Gaps
- BMI and its limits (adult categories 18.5–24.9 etc.; Asian, Black and older-adult caveats; waist-to-height ratio under 0.5 per NICE 2022) **[unverified this session]**. Sources: [WHO obesity fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/obesity-and-overweight), [NHS BMI](https://www.nhs.uk/health-assessment-tools/calculate-your-body-mass-index/), NICE.
- Supplements: the UK advises 10 µg vitamin D in autumn and winter and 400 µg folic acid before and during early pregnancy **[unverified]**. US: the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements is public domain. SA: there is no SAHPRA consumer guide found. Not researched.
- SA-specific breastfeeding policy (Tshwane Declaration 2011; the SA Code on breast-milk substitutes, R991 of 2012) **[unverified]**.
- GLP-1 weight-loss drugs and "food noise": not researched; probably belongs in a health course instead.

## Eating well on a tight budget and cooking basics; what surveys say adults wish they'd learnt

### Takeaway
I found weak survey evidence. A UK figure (only 6% of Brits say they learnt to cook nutritious meals at school) comes from a campaign Facebook post. Peer-reviewed US data shows self-rated cooking skill in early adulthood predicts better diet later. WHO explicitly endorses teaching cooking in schools. I fetched no budget-eating sources.

### Cited Findings
- About 25% of young adults rated their cooking skills "very adequate" and 56% "adequate". Those rating "very adequate" were more likely, years later, to prepare meals with vegetables and eat less fast food. — [Utter et al., PMC6086120](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6086120/)
- "Only 6% of Brits say they learned to cook nutritious meals at school", despite cooking being compulsory for ages 5–14. 97% of parents say cooking is an essential life skill. — [Best Food Forward campaign, Facebook (secondary; original survey not located)](https://www.facebook.com/BestFoodForwardProject/posts/97-of-parents-say-cooking-is-an-essential-life-skill-but-fewer-than-half-of-youn/854222487674092/)
- Confidence and self-efficacy matter as much as technical cooking skill. — [Johns Hopkins Public Health magazine, 2024](https://magazine.publichealth.jhu.edu/2024/cooking-skills-missing-ingredient-nutrition-efforts)
- WHO lists "encouraging culinary skills, including in children through schools" and adult nutrition education among recommended actions. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)
- SA's food guide is built from "local and affordable foods". Beans, lentils, soya, maas and starchy staples are budget anchors. — [FAO SA](https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/regions/countries/south-africa/en/)

### Inferences
- The budget lesson for SA can lean on the FBDG's affordable groups (pulses, maas, eggs, starch staples, seasonal veg) and on swapping sugary drinks for water. That swap saves money and avoids the HPL.

### Gaps
- No reliable large survey of "adults wish school taught X about food" was found. Candidates to check: YouGov UK, the Food Foundation, the BNF "Healthy Eating Week" surveys, and in the US, Pew or the IFIC Food & Health Survey (annual).
- SA food prices and budget data: the PMBEJD Household Affordability Index (monthly) is the standard SA source for basket costs **[unverified this session]**. A US source is USDA's "Thrifty Food Plan" / MyPlate Kitchen recipes (public domain). For the UK, the Food Foundation and NHS Healthier Families recipes.
- Cooking basics content (knife safety, a few staple recipes, batch cooking): no authoritative source gathered.
