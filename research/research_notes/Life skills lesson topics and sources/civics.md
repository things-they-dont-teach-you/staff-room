# Civics and politics: lesson topics and sources (SA first, then UK, US)

Researched 4 November 2026, which is SA's local government election day. Everything here is non-partisan: it covers process and rights, never parties or policy positions. Facts marked **[unverified this session]** come from the primary document but were not re-fetched; check them before publishing.

## Prioritised candidate lessons

### Takeaway
The lessons that pay off most are the ones adults need under pressure or on a deadline: arrest rights, registering and voting, reporting service-delivery failures, small claims, and PAIA/POPIA. The structural lessons (spheres of government, how laws are made) support those, so they come second.

### Cited Findings
- Demand signal: only 29% of South Africans are satisfied with how democracy works, and 49% prefer democracy to any other system (down from 72% in 2011). Disengagement is the problem these lessons aim at. — [Afrobarometer AD1170](https://www.afrobarometer.org/publication/ad1170-democracy-on-shaky-ground-in-south-africa-as-dissatisfaction-with-its-functioning-abounds/)
- South Africans want MPs to oversee the president and to listen to constituents, but report low trust in Parliament. That supports a "how to contact and use your representatives" lesson. — [Afrobarometer AD921](https://www.afrobarometer.org/publication/ad921-low-on-trust-and-satisfaction-south-africans-want-their-mps-to-listen/)

### Inferences
Proposed order for SA. It is also the template for UK and US.
1. **Your rights if arrested or stopped by police**: s35, the 48-hour rule, silence, bail, complaints.
2. **Registering and voting**: IEC registration, the ballots for each election type, and how PR turns votes into seats.
3. **Local government in practice**: your ward councillor and ward committee, the IDP, the budget and rates, and how to log and escalate a service fault.
4. **Getting money back without a lawyer**: Small Claims Court, now up to R30 000, and the letter of demand.
5. **Information and privacy rights**: PAIA requests, POPIA, and the Information Regulator.
6. **Work disputes**: CCMA basics.
7. **How government is built**: three spheres, NA and NCOP, the Constitution.
8. **How a law is made and how to comment on a bill**: public participation.
9. **Who to complain to**: Chapter 9 institutions (Public Protector, SAHRC and others).
10. **Media literacy and misinformation**, especially around elections.

### Gaps
- No ranked survey was found that asks SA adults "what civics do you wish school had taught you". The order above is inferred from urgency and how often each situation comes up.

## How government works in SA (spheres, Parliament, law-making, Constitution, Chapter 9)

### Takeaway
Every point can be sourced to the Constitution on gov.za. PMG is the best plain-language tracker of bills and committees, and it is licensed CC BY 3.0 ZA, so the course can reuse its text with attribution.

### Cited Findings
- The Constitution's Bill of Rights (Chapter 2) is hosted in full on gov.za. Key sections:
  - s32(1): "Everyone has the right of access to any information held by the state…"
  - s33(1): "Everyone has the right to administrative action that is lawful, reasonable and procedurally fair."
  - s14: right to privacy

  — [gov.za Constitution Ch 2](https://www.gov.za/documents/constitution/chapter-2-bill-rights)
- PMG content is "licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 South Africa license". Users "must indicate that the content is from the Parliamentary Monitoring Group www.pmg.org.za". — [PMG Disclaimer & Copyright](https://pmg.org.za/page/disclaimer-copyright)
- PMG publishes detailed, unofficial minutes of all parliamentary committee meetings. — [OpenUp: PMG](https://openup.org.za/projects/pmg)
- Structural facts to teach **[unverified this session; source is the Constitution, Chs 3–4, 7, 9, at gov.za/documents/constitution]**:
  - Government has three "distinctive, interdependent and interrelated" spheres (s40).
  - The NA has 350–400 members; it currently has 400.
  - The NCOP has 90 delegates, 10 per province, plus up to 10 part-time SALGA representatives.
  - Bills are tagged s75 (ordinary, not affecting provinces), s76 (affecting provinces), s77 (money bills) or s74 (constitutional amendments).
  - s59 and s72 require public involvement in NA and NCOP processes.
  - Chapter 9 institutions: Public Protector, SAHRC, CRL Rights Commission, Commission for Gender Equality, Auditor-General, Electoral Commission.

### Inferences
- The lesson on "how to comment on a bill" can point to parliament.gov.za calls for comment and to PMG's bill tracker. Using PMG text is safe under CC BY with the required attribution line.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch parliament.gov.za's or gov.za's copyright terms. SA legislation itself is not protected by copyright (Copyright Act s12(8)(a) **[unverified this session]**), but site text may be.
- I did not verify the current Public Protector's name or the SAHRC complaint-form URL. Avoid naming office-holders in lessons, because they change.

## Voting in SA (IEC registration, three ballots, PR, 2026 local elections)

### Takeaway
National elections have used three ballots since 2024. Local elections use two ballots in metros and three in local municipalities (ward, PR, district PR). The 2026 local government elections take place today, 4 November 2026, so lessons should be written from 5 November onwards and cover results and the five-year term.

### Cited Findings
- The President announced Wednesday 4 November 2026 as the local government election date. — [IEC news](https://www.elections.org.za/content/About-Us/News/Electoral-Commission-welcomes-announcement-of-4-November-2026-as-Local-Government-Elections-date/)
- The final voters' roll for the 2026 local elections was certified on 26 August 2026. — [IEC timetable](https://www.elections.org.za/pw/News-And-Media/News-List/News/News-Article/Electoral-Commission-publishes-the-2026-LGE-Election-Timetable?a=AISDGvpz75ps1usOfX7oilaW+/86AfW3IllIjMkcvPs=)
- Special votes were cast on 2 and 3 November 2026.
- Metro voters get a ward ballot and a PR ballot. Voters in local municipalities also get a district PR ballot.
- Accepted ID: smart ID card, green barcoded ID book, or a valid temporary identity certificate.

  — [SAnews, 31 Oct 2026](https://www.sanews.gov.za/south-africa/ready-vote-preparing-2026-local-government-elections)
- The IEC promoted online voter registration ahead of the 2026 polls. — [eNCA via Wikipedia ref](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_South_African_municipal_elections) (secondary; the primary is the IEC online registration portal)
- The Electoral Amendment Act 2023 introduced three national/provincial ballots from 2024:
  - **compensatory** ballot: 200 NA seats, parties only, closed lists
  - **regional** ballot: 200 NA seats split among the 9 provinces, parties plus independents
  - **provincial** legislature ballot

  — [IEC: What's new in 2024](https://www.elections.org.za/pw/elections/whats-new-in-the-2024-elections-electoral-amendment-act)
- Rules for independent candidates (same IEC page):
  - can win at most one NA seat, and any surplus seat is forfeited
  - must reside in the province they contest
  - need 1 000 supporter signatures (incumbents are exempt)
  - must pay a deposit
- How PR seats are worked out (same IEC page):
  - Droop-style quota = (valid votes ÷ seats) + 1, ignoring fractions.
  - Seats = votes ÷ quota.
  - Remaining seats go by largest remainder (up to 5 at national level), then by highest average.
- In 2024, 27.79 million registered voters received three ballots. — [SAnews guide](https://www.sanews.gov.za/south-africa/your-guide-vote-using-three-ballot-papers)

### Inferences
- The local election ballots (ward plus PR) and the national ones (three ballots) confuse people, so a side-by-side table is a strong lesson asset.
- The quota formula above works as a hands-on "count the seats yourself" exercise.

### Gaps
- IEC copyright terms: the Copyright-Notice URL redirected to the IEC homepage, so I could not read the reuse terms. Treat IEC content as all-rights-reserved and link to it rather than copy. Asking the IEC directly is advisable.
- 2026 turnout and final registered-voter numbers are not available yet; it is election day.

## Your rights in SA (police and arrest, PAIA, POPIA, CCMA, small claims, complaints)

### Takeaway
The arrest-rights text is short and can be quoted verbatim from the Constitution. The Small Claims Court limit rose to **R30 000 from 1 August 2026**, so older content that says R20 000 is out of date. PAIA complaints go to the Information Regulator on Form 5 within 180 days.

### Cited Findings
- **Arrest rights, s35(1).** Anyone arrested has the right to:
  - remain silent
  - be told promptly of that right and of the consequences of not remaining silent
  - not be compelled to confess
  - be brought before a court within 48 hours, or by the end of the first court day after that if the 48 hours end outside court hours
  - at the first appearance, be charged, told why detention continues, or released
  - be released if the interests of justice permit

  — [gov.za Constitution Ch 2](https://www.gov.za/documents/constitution/chapter-2-bill-rights)
- **Small claims:**
  - Limit is R30 000, up from R20 000, effective 1 August 2026 (GG 55038, GoN 7717, 20 July 2026).
  - No lawyer is needed.
  - Process: ask informally, then send a letter of demand with 14 days to pay, then go to the clerk at the Magistrate's Court, who issues a summons. A Commissioner hears the case. If the other side doesn't pay, the matter goes to the magistrate's court for execution.

  — [DoJ&CD Small Claims Courts](https://www.justice.gov.za/scc/scc.htm); [DoJ&CD statement, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.justice.gov.za/m_statements/2026/20260722-SCC-Monetary-Jurisdiction-R30000.html)
- **PAIA:**
  - If a request is refused or ignored, you can complain to the Information Regulator in writing on Form 5, within 180 days (condonation is possible after that).
  - The Regulator publishes a PAIA guide and the request forms.

  — [Information Regulator PAIA](https://inforegulator.org.za/paia/); [Complaints](https://inforegulator.org.za/complaints/)
- PAIA requests must be made on a form that "corresponds substantially with Form 2" (Regulation 7.1, 2021 Regulations). — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/south-africa/privacy/information-regulator-cracks-down-on-outdated-paia-request-forms-%E2%80%93-what-every-information-officer-needs-to-know) (secondary)
- POPIA complaints must now be submitted through the Information Regulator's eServices portal. — [Information Regulator (Facebook)](https://www.facebook.com/InformationRegulatorSA/posts/popia-complaints-must-now-be-submitted-through-the-information-regulators-eservi/1285339707116172/) (official account, but confirm on inforegulator.org.za)

### Inferences
- A practical "what to say if arrested" card is possible, but it must say this is not legal advice and must point to Legal Aid SA.

### Gaps

  - CCMA referral time limits (30 days for an unfair dismissal, 90 days for an unfair labour practice **[unverified this session]**; source is ccma.org.za / LRA s191)
  - IPID complaint process and Legal Aid SA eligibility
  - the Public Protector and SAHRC complaint routes

## UK equivalents (Parliament, Electoral Commission, voter ID, MPs, councils)

### Takeaway
UK voter ID (Elections Act 2022) is in force. The **Representation of the People Bill**, which would bring votes at 16, a registration age of 14, bank cards as ID and tighter donation rules, is **still a Bill, at Lords Committee Stage as of 2 Nov 2026**. Lessons must not describe it as law. UK Parliament content is reusable, commercially too, under the Open Parliament Licence v3.0.

### Cited Findings
- Voter ID:
  - There are more than 20 accepted photo IDs (passport, driving licence, bus passes, Veteran Card and others).
  - The free Voter Authority Certificate covers people with none.

  — [Electoral Commission: accepted ID](https://www.electoralcommission.org.uk/voting-and-elections/voter-id/accepted-forms-photo-id); [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cm2lgnze013o)
- The Representation of the People Bill:
  - extends the vote to 16 and 17-year-olds in all UK elections
  - lowers the registration age to 14
  - accepts UK bank cards as ID (physical card, in date, name matching the register)
  - requires digital ID to be visually verifiable
  - caps overseas-elector donations at £100 000 a year and bans crypto donations
  - is intended to be in force "in time for the next General Election"

  It entered Lords Committee Stage around 2 Nov 2026. — [MHCLG blog, 2 Nov 2026](https://mhclgmedia.blog.gov.uk/2026/11/02/the-representation-of-the-people-bill-lords-committee-stage/); [GOV.UK policy summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/representation-of-the-people-bill-policy-summaries/voter-identification); [Bill page](https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/4080)
- Votes at 16 would add about 1.7 million voters. — [Electoral Commission briefing](https://www.electoralcommission.org.uk/news-and-views/representation-people-bill/briefing-representation-people-bill-lords-second-reading)
- **Open Parliament Licence v3.0:**
  - You may copy, adapt and use the information commercially.
  - Attribution must read: "Contains Parliamentary information licensed under the Open Parliament Licence v3.0."
  - Excluded: personal data, the Royal Arms and Crowned Portcullis, third-party rights.

  — [OPL](https://www.parliament.uk/site-information/copyright-parliament/open-parliament-licence/)

### Inferences
- Use the Commons Library progress tracker ([CBP-10876](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10876/)) as the live source, and re-check the Bill's status before publishing.

### Gaps
- Not fetched this session:
  - OGL v3.0 terms for gov.uk (widely known to be similar to OPL **[unverified this session]**)
  - Electoral Commission website reuse terms
  - "write to your MP" (WriteToThem/parliament.uk) and council structure (unitary vs two-tier) sources
- Voter ID requirements differ across the UK nations (NI has had photo ID longer; Scottish and Welsh devolved elections have different rules) **[unverified this session]**.

## US equivalents (federal/state/local, registration, contacting representatives)

### Takeaway
Federal government works are public domain, so usa.gov, vote.gov and congress.gov content can be reused freely. Voter registration is run by each state, so lessons should send people to vote.gov rather than state any one deadline.

### Cited Findings
- vote.gov is the official registration entry point; it routes by state and is available in many languages. — [vote.gov](https://vote.gov/)
- USAGov explains copyright on federal government materials (works of the US government are not protected by copyright, 17 USC §105). — [USAGov: government copyright](https://www.usa.gov/government-copyright)
- USAGov lists state and local election offices for registration questions. — [USAGov election offices](https://www.usa.gov/state-election-office)
- The DOJ Civil Rights Division explains the federal laws on police misconduct and how to report it. — [DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/crt/addressing-police-misconduct-laws-enforced-department-justice)
- The ACLU "Stopped by Police" know-your-rights guide (ACLU is an NGO; check its licence before reusing). — [ACLU](https://www.aclu.org/know-your-rights/stopped-by-police)

### Inferences
- §105 covers federal works only. State government sites vary, and some claim copyright.

## Survey data on civic knowledge gaps

### Takeaway
Afrobarometer Round 10 (2025) gives strong attitude data for SA. HSRC SASAS has run every year since 2003 and covers democracy and governance, but I found no published civic-knowledge quiz result from it.

### Cited Findings
- Afrobarometer Round 10 South Africa: 1 600 adults, interviewed 28 June – 19 July 2025 by the Institute for Justice and Reconciliation. — [Afrobarometer R10 summary](https://www.afrobarometer.org/publication/south-africa-round-10-summary-of-results/)
- Results from that survey:
  - 49% prefer democracy (60% in 2000, 72% in 2011, 40% in 2021); SA ranks 3rd-lowest of 38 countries.
  - 61% say elections are the best way to choose leaders.
  - 31% call SA a full democracy or one with minor problems.
  - 29% are satisfied with democracy; SA ranks 10th-highest for dissatisfaction.
  - 40% say parties are divisive (25% in 2011).

  — [AD1170](https://www.afrobarometer.org/publication/ad1170-democracy-on-shaky-ground-in-south-africa-as-dissatisfaction-with-its-functioning-abounds/); [PP102](https://www.afrobarometer.org/publication/pp102-democracy-and-its-discontented-what-drives-demand-for-democracy-in-south-africa/)
- Afrobarometer 2024–25 microdata is available via UCT DataFirst. — [DataFirst](https://www.datafirst.uct.ac.za/afrobarometer-survey-2024-2025)
- HSRC SASAS is a nationally representative survey run every year since 2003. Its 2020 questionnaire covers democracy and governance. — [HSRC SASAS](https://hsrc.ac.za/special-projects/sasas/); [SASAS 2020 Q1](https://hsrc-repository.figshare.com/articles/dataset/South_African_Social_Attitudes_Survey_SASAS_2020_Questionnaire_1_-_All_provinces/28731701)

### Inferences
- Afrobarometer measures attitudes, not knowledge. The case for "knowledge gaps" in the course should be framed as low trust and low engagement, not as measured ignorance.

### Gaps
- No SA civic-knowledge test data was found (for example, "can you name your ward councillor").
- Afrobarometer's licence or reuse terms for its charts were not checked; citing its statistics with a link is safe.
- UK sources (Hansard Society Audit of Political Engagement) and US sources (Annenberg Constitution Day civics survey) were not fetched.
