# Teach the deadlines, the numbers, and the help lines

The lessons most worth building first are the ones adults need under pressure or on a deadline, with a primary source to cite. In **tax**, the SA course should first be refreshed against Budget 2026, which made the first inflation adjustment to brackets since 2023/24 and moved almost every threshold a lesson touches. After that it should add lessons on first-job tax numbers, savings tax (interest, TFSA, CGT) and two-pot withdrawals. In **money**, compound interest is the most-failed literacy question worldwide, the emergency fund is the most common gap, and credit costs are the biggest harm. In **civics**, the order is arrest rights, registering and voting, and local government. In **health**, start with emergency numbers and normal vital signs. In **nutrition**, start with the healthy plate and reading a label. Licensing splits sharply by country. US federal material (IRS, CFPB, CDC, FDA, USDA) is public domain. UK gov.uk, NHS and Parliament content is reusable with attribution under the Open Government or Open Parliament licences. WHO material is non-commercial share-alike. Most South African regulator content (SARS, FSCA, NCR, IEC, Department of Health) has no reuse licence that this research could find, so the SA-first course will mostly paraphrase and link. Several facts changed in the last few weeks: the UK cash-ISA cut was cancelled on 28 Oct 2026, the SA small-claims limit rose on 1 Aug 2026, and SA held local elections today. Large areas are still unverified, above all every first-aid step and the UK and US crisis lines. Those need a sourcing pass before any lesson goes live.

## Budget 2026 changed most numbers in the SA tax course

The existing six-lesson SA course (brackets and rebates, payslip, filing season, deductions vs credits, provisional tax, SARS scams) was fact-checked on 2 Nov 2026 for the 2027 year of assessment. It should now be checked line by line against SARS's Budget 2026 FAQ. The **2026/27 brackets rose 3.4%, the first inflation relief since 2023/24**: **18% to R245,100; 26% to R383,100; 31% to R530,200; 36% to R695,800; 39% to R887,000; 41% to R1,878,600; 45% above** ([SARS Budget 2026 FAQ](https://www.sars.gov.za/about/sars-tax-and-customs-system/budget/budget-2026-frequently-asked-questions/)). The other figures to check are as follows:

- **Rebates** are **R17,820 primary, R9,765 secondary and R3,249 tertiary**. The tax-free thresholds are **R99,000** under 65, R153,250 for ages 65–74 and R171,300 for 75 and over.
- **Medical credits** are **R376/month** for each of the first two people covered and **R254** for each additional dependant.
- The **retirement deduction cap rose from R350,000 to R430,000**, the first change since 2016 ([SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/about/sars-tax-and-customs-system/budget/budget-2026-frequently-asked-questions/)).

For the provisional-tax lesson, the rules to confirm are the **20% under-estimation penalty** (triggered when the second estimate is below 80% of final taxable income) and the exemption tests. You are exempt only if you run no business and either earn under R99,000, or earn only interest, dividends, rental or foreign income of R30,000 or less. For the filing-season lesson, confirm the **10.25% late-payment interest rate from 2 March 2026** and SARS's power to issue a **third-party appointment (AA88)**, which lets it take a tax debt directly from your bank or employer without a court order ([SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/about/sars-tax-and-customs-system/budget/budget-2026-frequently-asked-questions/)). One caution: the FAQ page itself contains garbled fuel-levy figures. Cross-check every lesson number against the [Budget Tax Guide 2026 PDF](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Docs/Budget/Budget2026/Budget-tax-guide-2026-web-version.pdf) rather than trusting the FAQ alone.

The same budget creates the case for new lessons. Proposed priority order:

1. **Getting a tax number for a first job.** From the 202602 reconciliation period, eFiling rejects an employer's EMP501 if a qualifying employee has no income tax reference number on their IRP5. The national minimum wage is **R30.23/hour** from 2 March 2026 ([SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/about/sars-tax-and-customs-system/budget/budget-2026-frequently-asked-questions/)).
2. **Saving and investing tax.** Interest is exempt up to **R23,800** (R34,500 for those 65+). The **TFSA annual limit rose from R36,000 to R46,000** from 1 March 2026; the lifetime limit stays at R500,000, and excess contributions are taxed at 40%. The **CGT annual exclusion rose to R50,000** and the **primary-residence exclusion to R3m** ([SARS Tax Free Investments](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/personal-income-tax/tax-free-investments/); [2026 Budget Speech](https://archive.treasury.gov.za/documents/national%20budget/2026/speech/speech.pdf)).
3. **Two-pot withdrawals.** These are taxed at your marginal rate under a tax directive. Any shortfall shows up on the auto-assessment issued around 21 July 2026, with payment due by 20 October 2026. That surprise bill is exactly what a lesson should prevent.
4. **Side income, gig work and crypto.** Crypto traded frequently or in a business-like way is ordinary income; crypto held long-term is subject to CGT. Exchanges began **CARF reporting** to SARS on 2 March 2026.
5. **Owing SARS and debt relief.**
6. **Working abroad.** The first **R1.25m** of foreign employment income is exempt if you pass the 183-day and 60-day test ([SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/individuals/tax-during-all-life-stages-and-events/foreign-employment-income-exemption/)).
7. **Micro-business VAT and turnover tax.** Compulsory VAT registration now applies above **R2.3m** of turnover, up from R1m.
8. **Deceased estates.**

The US evidence explains why the course should open with bracket mechanics. In Tax Foundation polling, only **28% of people earning under $25k** understood how brackets work, against 66% of those earning $100–150k. Only **24% of respondents with high-school education** could tell a deduction from a credit ([Tax Foundation](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/federal/us-tax-literacy-poll-knowledge-perceptions/)). This research found no SA or UK equivalent survey.

The **UK tax course** should run in this order:

1. **Personal allowance and bands.** The allowance is £12,570. Basic rate is 20% to £50,270, higher rate 40% to £125,140, additional rate 45% above that. The allowance tapers above £100k ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/income-tax-rates)). It has been **frozen since 2021/22 and stays frozen to 2030/31**, so teach fiscal drag explicitly ([Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10618/)).
2. **Payslip: PAYE, tax codes and NI.** Employee NI is **8% between £242 and £967 a week**.
3. **Student loans.** Repayment is **9% above the threshold**: Plan 2 £29,385, Plan 5 £25,000 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027)). That gives a Plan 2 graduate an effective marginal rate of about 37%.
4. **The £1,000 trading and property allowances.**
5. **Making Tax Digital.** It has been mandatory since 6 April 2026 for self-employment plus property income above **£50k**. The threshold falls to £30k in 2027 and £20k in 2028 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/sign-up-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)).
6. **ISAs.**
7. **HMRC scams.**

The **US tax course** should run in this order:

1. **W-4 and W-2 vs 1099.**
2. **Brackets and the standard deduction.** The 2026 standard deduction is **$16,100 single and $32,200 joint**. Single brackets start at 10% to $12,400 ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/working-families-tax-cuts-individuals-and-workers)).
3. **Credits.** The Child Tax Credit is up to **$2,200**, of which $1,700 is refundable ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/child-tax-credit)). The maximum EITC is **$8,231** ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)).
4. **Free filing.** **Direct File is gone.** The free routes left are Free File (AGI ≤ $89,000), Fillable Forms and VITA ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/2026-tax-filing-season-opens-with-several-free-filing-options-available); [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/it-modernization/2025/11/irs-direct-file-will-not-be-available-in-2026-agency-tells-states/)).
5. **State vs federal tax.**
6. **Gig work and quarterly estimated tax.**
7. **The 2025–2028-only tips and overtime deductions.**
8. **Scams.** The IRS 2026 "Dirty Dozen" list includes AI voice-clone robocalls and ghost preparers. The IRS usually makes first contact by mail ([IRS IR-2026-30](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/dirty-dozen-tax-scams-for-2026-irs-reminds-taxpayers-to-watch-out-for-dangerous-threats)).

## Compound interest and credit costs anchor the money course

The survey data sets the order. Across 39 countries in the OECD/INFE 2023 survey:

- **only 42% answered the compound-interest question correctly**
- **only 43% could cover three months' living costs** without income
- **15% had been scammed**
- **only 37% check whether a provider is regulated** ([OECD/INFE 2023](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/12/oecd-infe-2023-international-survey-of-adult-financial-literacy_8ce94e2c/56003a32-en.pdf))

South Africa's own baseline is weaker still: an average **52/100**, with every domain declining ([FSCA 2020 baseline](https://www.fsca.co.za/_api/cr3ad_newses(418d9578-f85f-f111-a826-000d3a64fd63)/cr3ad_document/$value)). The money course should therefore run in this order:

1. compound interest in both directions (saving and debt)
2. budgeting and an emergency fund
3. borrowing costs and legal caps
4. credit reports
5. scams and checking the regulator
6. getting out of debt
7. retirement and tax-free savings
8. inflation and index funds
9. consumer rights
10. payslips and UIF
11. renting

**Borrowing costs** is the most South-African lesson in the set. The National Credit Regulations cap interest relative to the repo rate, fixed at the rate in force when the credit is granted:

| Type of credit | Maximum interest |
|---|---|
| Mortgages | repo + 12% a year |
| Credit cards and store cards | repo + 14% a year |
| Unsecured loans | repo + 21% a year |
| Developmental credit | repo + 27% a year |
| Short-term loans | 5% a month on the first loan, 3% on later loans in the same year |

Fees are capped too. For unsecured loans the initiation fee is at most R165 plus 10% of the amount above R1,000, up to R1,050. The monthly service fee is at most **R60** ([GG 39379](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201511/39379gon1080.pdf)). Every lender must now register with the NCR, so "ask for their NCR number" works as a test for spotting an illegal mashonisa. That test comes from a law firm's summary ([MJK Inc](https://mjkinc.co.za/finance-credit-law/interest-and-the-in-duplum-rule)) and should be confirmed with the NCR. The prescribed interest rate rose to **10.25% from 1 March 2026** ([GG 54520](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202604/54520gen3887.pdf)). That implies a repo rate of 6.75% then, which would put the store-card cap near 20.75% and the unsecured-loan cap near 27.75%. Recompute both from the live repo rate before publishing.

**Credit reports** are a free legal right in all three countries:

- **South Africa:** one free report a year from each bureau, yet **fewer than 5% of consumers** use it ([TransUnion SA](https://www.transunion.co.za/assistance/free-credit-report); [NCR](https://ncr.org.za/documents/pages/Brochures/NCR_%20english%20.pdf)).
- **United States:** **free weekly reports, made permanent in October 2023**, available only through AnnualCreditReport.com ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/10/you-now-have-permanent-access-free-weekly-credit-reports)).
- **United Kingdom:** the statutory free report was not verified.

**Retirement and saving** facts to teach:

- **SA two-pot:** savings-pot withdrawals need at least **R2,000** in the pot, are limited to **once per tax year**, and are taxed at your marginal rate ([FSCA explainer](https://www.fsca.co.za/_api/cr3ad_shareddocumentses(d36a1ed9-0c6b-f111-a826-000d3a64f818)/cr3ad_document/$value); [SARS calculator](https://tools.sars.gov.za/sarsonlinequery/savings-pot-calculator)).
- **US 2026 limits:** **$24,500** for a 401(k) and **$7,500** for an IRA ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500)).

**Consumer rights and payslips** facts to teach:

- **CPA:** direct-marketing sales carry a **5-business-day cooling-off** period, and defective goods can be returned within **6 months** ([dtic CPA booklet](https://www.thedtic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Consumer_Protection_Act.pdf)).
- **UIF:** you earn 1 credit day for every 4 days worked, up to 365. Benefits pay a sliding **38–60%** of earnings for the first 238 credit days, then 20% ([Dept of Employment and Labour](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Publications/Unemployment%20Insurance%20Fund/Fact%20Sheet%20on%20the%20Calculation%20of%20normal%20UIF%20benefit.pdf)).

## Arrest rights, voting and service delivery come before civics structure

SA's civic problem is disengagement more than ignorance. Only **29% are satisfied with how democracy works**, and support for democracy has fallen to **49%, from 72% in 2011** ([Afrobarometer AD1170](https://www.afrobarometer.org/publication/ad1170-democracy-on-shaky-ground-in-south-africa-as-dissatisfaction-with-its-functioning-abounds/)). Lessons should therefore start with the situations where knowing the rules changes the outcome. Recommended order:

1. arrest rights
2. registering and voting
3. local government and logging service faults
4. small claims
5. PAIA and POPIA
6. CCMA
7. the three spheres of government and Parliament
8. commenting on a bill
9. Chapter 9 institutions
10. election misinformation

Facts to teach in the first lessons:

- **Arrest rights** can be quoted from s35 of the Constitution: the right to remain silent, not to be compelled to confess, and to be brought to court **within 48 hours**, or by the end of the next court day if the 48 hours end outside court hours ([gov.za Constitution Ch 2](https://www.gov.za/documents/constitution/chapter-2-bill-rights)).
- **Small claims:** the limit rose from **R20,000 to R30,000 on 1 August 2026**, so older material is now wrong. No lawyer is needed; the first step is a letter of demand giving 14 days to pay ([DoJ&CD](https://www.justice.gov.za/m_statements/2026/20260722-SCC-Monetary-Jurisdiction-R30000.html)).
- **PAIA:** if a request is refused or ignored, complain to the Information Regulator on **Form 5 within 180 days** ([Information Regulator](https://inforegulator.org.za/paia/)).

**Voting.** Today, **4 November 2026, is the local government election** ([IEC](https://www.elections.org.za/content/About-Us/News/Electoral-Commission-welcomes-announcement-of-4-November-2026-as-Local-Government-Elections-date/)). Metro voters get a ward ballot and a PR ballot; voters in local municipalities also get a district PR ballot ([SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/south-africa/ready-vote-preparing-2026-local-government-elections)). National elections have used three ballots since 2024. The IEC's seat-quota formula, (valid votes ÷ seats) + 1, makes a good hands-on "count the seats yourself" exercise ([IEC](https://www.elections.org.za/pw/elections/whats-new-in-the-2024-elections-electoral-amendment-act)). Ward boundaries changed for 2026, so lessons must tell learners to look up their ward rather than assume it.

**UK.** Photo voter ID is in force, with more than 20 accepted IDs and a free Voter Authority Certificate for people without one ([Electoral Commission](https://www.electoralcommission.org.uk/voting-and-elections/voter-id/accepted-forms-photo-id)). **Votes at 16 is still only a Bill**, at Lords Committee Stage as of 2 Nov 2026, so lessons must not describe it as law ([MHCLG blog](https://mhclgmedia.blog.gov.uk/2026/11/02/the-representation-of-the-people-bill-lords-committee-stage/)).

**US.** Registration varies by state, so send learners to [vote.gov](https://vote.gov/) rather than quoting any one state's deadline.

## Emergency numbers and vital signs lead health; first aid is unsourced

**Emergency numbers** come first because they are the cheapest lesson to teach and the most valuable.

| Country | Numbers | Source |
|---|---|---|
| South Africa | **10111** police; **10177** ambulance; **112** from any mobile; GBV Command Centre **0800 428 428**; Western Cape poisons line **0861 555 777** | [Western Cape Government](https://www.westerncape.gov.za/know-who-you-can-call-emergency); GBV line from a [GovernmentZA social post](https://www.facebook.com/GovernmentZA/posts/-save-these-emergency-numbers-share-this-and-help-save-a-lifegovzaupdates/1184733833692415/) |
| UK | **999** for emergencies; **NHS 111** when unsure whether you need A&E | [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-go-to-ae/) (the 999 primary page was not fetched) |
| US | **911** | [AHA](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings) |

No national SA Department of Health page confirming these numbers was found.

**Normal vital signs for adults:**

| Measure | Normal range | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | fever is **38°C or above** | [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/fever-in-adults/) |
| Resting pulse | **60–100 bpm** | [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002341.htm) |
| Breathing | **12–18/min** per MedlinePlus, 12–20 per the [American Lung Association](https://www.lung.org/blog/respiratory-rate-vital-signs); teach "about 12–20" | as listed |
| Oxygen saturation | **95–100%** | [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/pulse-oximetry/) |

**Fever in children** is also 38°C or above. Call NHS 111 for a baby **under 3 months at 38°C or above**, or **3–6 months at 39°C or above**. Call 999 for any of these red flags ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/fever-in-children/)):

- a rash that does not fade under a pressed glass
- a stiff neck
- a first febrile fit
- grunting breathing
- blue, grey or blotchy skin. The NHS notes this "may be harder to see on brown or black skin", which matters for an SA audience.

**Blood pressure is where countries disagree.** The NHS calls clinic readings of **140/90 or higher** high, and home readings of **135/85 or higher** ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/high-blood-pressure/)). The American Heart Association bands are:

| AHA category | Reading |
|---|---|
| Normal | under 120/80 |
| Stage 1 hypertension | **130–139 or 80–89** |
| Stage 2 | 140 or more, or 90 or more |
| Severe | **above 180/120**: re-measure after a minute; call **911** if it comes with chest pain, breathlessness, weakness or speech change |

Source: [AHA](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings). A reading of 134/84 is "not high" in the UK but "stage 1" in the US, so the lesson should teach the cut-offs that learners' own doctors use. SA-specific thresholds were not found.

**Medicines.** For adult paracetamol: no more than **8 × 500 mg tablets in 24 hours**, at least **4 hours apart**, and never alongside cold-and-flu remedies that also contain paracetamol ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/paracetamol-for-adults/)).

**Mental health.** SADAG's suicide crisis line, **0800 567 567**, is confirmed on SADAG's own site ([SADAG](https://www.sadag.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=11&Itemid=114)). The WhatsApp number (076 882 2775) and SMS 31393 appear only in government social-media posts. **Samaritans (116 123) and US 988 were not verified.**

**Sexual health (SA).** TB tests are free at public clinics. **Injectable lenacapavir PrEP**, given twice a year, has been rolling out in public facilities since June 2026. In the Western Cape, nearly **9 in 10 recipients were first-time PrEP users** ([Western Cape Government](https://www.westerncape.gov.za/health-wellness/article/nearly-9-10-people-choosing-lenacapavir-western-cape-are-first-time-users-long-acting-hiv)).

**First aid has proven demand but no verified content.** In a 2026 Red Cross survey, **80% of US adults** said CPR and AED training matters, but only **25% feel confident** acting, and **half wrongly believe** you need training to use an AED ([American Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org/about-us/news-and-events/news/2026/we-know-cpr-saves-lives-so-why-don-t-we-do-it.html)). Yet **no step was verified**: not compression rate or depth, choking, bleeding, how long to cool a burn, the recovery position, FAST stroke signs or heart-attack signs. The Resuscitation Council UK URL that was tried returned a 404. Source this lesson from resus.org.uk, the Resuscitation Council of Southern Africa and the AHA/ERC 2025 guidelines before writing a word.

## Healthy plate and food labels lead nutrition, with WHO as referee

Every guide agrees on the core: mostly whole foods; plenty of vegetables, fruit, pulses and whole grains; and less salt, sugar and saturated fat. The WHO fact sheet, updated 26 Jan 2026, gives the cleanest numbers for a global default ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)):

| Nutrient | WHO guidance |
|---|---|
| Fruit and veg | **at least 400 g a day** |
| Fibre | **at least 25 g** |
| Free sugars | **under 10% of energy, about 50 g (12 teaspoons)**; fruit juice counts as free sugar |
| Salt | **under 5 g a day** |
| Saturated fat | **at most 10%** of energy |
| Protein | **10–15% of energy** |

WHO also advises against non-sugar sweeteners as a way to cut sugar.

**South Africa's guidelines** are 11 messages, such as "Eat dry beans, split peas, lentils and soya regularly" and "Use salt… sparingly". The SA food guide shows only foods to eat, not foods to limit. The guidelines date from **2012**, and a revision may be under way ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/regions/countries/south-africa/en/)). The UK caps free sugars at **30 g a day** for adults ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/food-types/how-does-sugar-in-our-diet-affect-our-health/)).

**The US 2025–2030 guidelines (January 2026) are contested and should be taught that way.** They say "no amount" of added sugar is recommended, cap added sugar at **10 g per meal**, and advise no added sugar for children under 10. They set protein at **1.2–1.6 g/kg**, which Harvard calls "protein hype". They group butter and tallow under "healthy fats", and the independent advisory committee's report was set aside ([Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/2026/01/09/dietary-guidelines-for-americans-2025-2030/)). The gap between the WHO and US protein figures makes a good myth-busting exercise.

**Food labels.** SA's **R146 (2010) is still the law**. Draft R3337, which would add Chile-style triangle warnings for high sugar, sodium, saturated fat and sweeteners, **is not final**, and campaigners want it approved by **10 Dec 2026** ([ASC Food Safety](https://ascfoodsafety.com/front-of-pack-and-regulatory-defence/); [Health-e News](https://health-e.org.za/2026/10/08/op-ed-south-africans-have-waited-long-enough-for-front-of-pack-warning-labels/)). The US FDA front-of-pack box is also **still only a proposed rule** ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/front-package-nutrition-labeling)). Teach "compare per 100 g" as the skill that works in every country, and build the SA lesson so the warning triangles can be switched in once R3337 is gazetted.

**Food safety at home.** Throw out leftovers left out for **more than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 32°C/90°F** ([USDA FSIS](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)). SA's **2017–18 listeriosis outbreak, traced to polony, was the world's largest**: 1,060 confirmed cases and 216 deaths ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6653791/); figures via [Wikipedia citing the NDoH report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%932018_South_African_listeriosis_outbreak)). That makes a strong local hook for a lesson on the groups most at risk, such as pregnant women.

**Sugar and salt policy.** SA's Health Promotion Levy is **2.1c per gram of sugar above 4 g/100 ml** ([SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/customs-and-excise/excise/health-promotion-levy-on-sugary-beverages/)). It cut sugar bought through taxed drinks by **33%** within two years ([UNU-WIDER](https://www.wider.unu.edu/publication/shifting-sweetness-impacts-south-africas-health-promotion-levy-sugar-sweetened)). SA's mandatory sodium limits (2016, tightened 2019) went with a fall in salt intake of about **1.2 g a day** ([PMC10641639](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10641639/)).

## Conclusion

The research shows the course is mainly a maintenance problem. Within one year, every subject has had a threshold move, a rule reversed or a regulation stuck in draft. Of the SA tax course's six lessons, at least four (brackets and rebates, payslip, filing season, deductions vs credits) depend on Budget 2026 figures, and provisional tax depends on figures that should be re-confirmed. The UK ISA reversal happened within days of the most recent source pages. Each lesson therefore needs a "facts as at" date, a named primary source and a re-check trigger tied to a budget or gazette. AI-tutored delivery makes that easier, because the facts can live in a single data file the tutor reads. It also makes it riskier, because a tutor answering confidently from stale numbers is worse than a printed booklet.

Licensing should decide the course's own licence before any content is written. If the repos reuse WHO material, they inherit NC-SA. If they want CC BY, they must write original text, cite WHO and NHS rather than adapt them, and lean on US public-domain and OGL sources. The SA-first audience is the hardest to serve on both counts. Its regulators publish without reuse licences, and its most urgent lessons (first aid, crisis lines, credit caps) have the weakest verification. The next sourcing pass should go there first.
