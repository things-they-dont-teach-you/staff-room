## Social Security: credits, full retirement age, claiming age, my Social Security, 2026 COLA, trust fund outlook

### Takeaway
You need 40 credits (about 10 years of work) to qualify, and in 2026 one credit takes $1,890 of earnings. For anyone born in 1960 or later, full retirement age is 67. Claiming at 62 pays about 70% of the full benefit, and waiting until 70 pays about 124%. The 2026 COLA is 2.8%. The 2026 Trustees Report says the combined trust funds run out in Q3 2034, after which ongoing taxes cover about 83% of scheduled benefits. The retirement-only (OASI) fund runs out sooner, in Q4 2032.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 COLA is **2.8%**, based on CPI-W from Q3 2024 to Q3 2025 **[verified]**: [SSA 2026 COLA fact sheet](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2026.html), confirmed by the [Federal Register notice, 3 Nov 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-11-03/pdf/2025-19763.pdf). The average increase is about $56/month per beneficiary, per [NCPSSM fast facts](https://www.ncpssm.org/documents/social-security-facts/fast-facts-about-social-security/) (an advocacy group, so treat as secondary).
- In 2026 one credit (a "quarter of coverage") takes **$1,890** of earnings (up from $1,810), so all four credits for the year take $7,560. Credits are capped at 4 per year, and retirement benefits need 40 **[verified, figure]**: [SSA 2026 fact sheet](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2026.html), [Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-11-03/pdf/2025-19763.pdf). The 40-credit rule is in [SSA "How You Earn Credits"](https://www.ssa.gov/pubs/EN-05-10072.pdf) **[not re-fetched]**.
- The 2026 taxable maximum (wage base) is **$184,500**, up from $176,100 **[verified]**: [SSA 2026 fact sheet](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2026.html).
- **2027 preview:** COLA 3.6%, quarter of coverage $1,970, taxable maximum $192,300. These come from a secondary source and have not been checked against SSA: [Society of Actuaries, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.soa.org/communities/retirement-practice/newsletter-articles/2026/october/2026-10-rp-schobel/). Check them at ssa.gov/cola before publishing.
- Full retirement age (FRA) rises gradually to **67 for people born in 1960 or later**: [SSA pamphlet, full retirement age](https://ssa.gov/international/Agreement_Pamphlets/full-retirement-age.html).
- For the FRA-67 group, claiming at **62 pays about 70%** of the primary insurance amount (a permanent 30% cut). Delaying to **70 pays about 124%**, from delayed retirement credits of 8% per year for 3 years: SSA's own page is at [ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/agereduction.html](https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/agereduction.html) but returned 403 here. Secondary confirmation: [Maximize My Social Security](https://www.maximizemysocialsecurity.com/blog/social-security-full-retirement-age-by-birth-year) and [q3adv](https://q3adv.com/social-security-full-retirement-age/). The 8% per year is the standard DRC rate for people born 1943 or later, but no fetched source stated it directly here.
- **2026 Trustees Report** (CRS summary dated 25 Jun 2026) **[verified]**: [CRS IF13256](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13256.html)
  - OASI (retirement) reserves are depleted in **Q4 2032**, earlier than the 2025 report's Q1 2033.
  - Combined OASDI reserves are depleted in **Q3 2034**, when about **83%** of scheduled benefits remain payable, falling to 65% by 2100.
  - DI is not depleted within 75 years.
  - The 75-year actuarial deficit is **4.42% of taxable payroll**, up from 3.82%.
- After OASI depletion in 2032, about 78% of scheduled retirement benefits would be payable, and combined reserves fell $160B in 2025 to $2.56T: [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/personal-finance/social-security-has-less-than-10-years-before-reserves-exhausted-new-trustees-report-warns) (secondary). Medicare HI is depleted in Q2 2033: [NTU](https://www.ntu.org/foundation/detail/social-security-and-medicare-trust-fund-depletion-dates-move-closer). The primary source is the Trustees Report at ssa.gov/oact/TR/2026.
- **my Social Security** (ssa.gov/myaccount) gives you your earnings record, your Social Security Statement and benefit estimates at 62, at FRA and at 70 **[not re-fetched]**: [SSA my Social Security](https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/).

### Inferences
- Lesson framing: "Social Security will not disappear. Under current law it pays roughly 77–83% of scheduled benefits after depletion, unless Congress acts." That is accurate and avoids doom framing.
- The 62 / 67 / 70 comparison (70% / 100% / 124%) is a strong single visual. Claiming at 70 pays about 77% more per month than claiming at 62.
- Lesson action item: open a my Social Security account and check your earnings record for errors.

## Workplace plans: 401(k)/403(b), match, vesting, Roth vs traditional, auto-enrollment, 2026 limits, rollovers, early withdrawal

### Takeaway
The 2026 limits are confirmed by IRS IR-2025-111:
- $24,500 employee deferral
- $8,000 catch-up at 50+ (total $32,500)
- $11,250 catch-up at ages 60–63

From 2026, people whose prior-year FICA wages from that employer were over $150,000 must make catch-ups as Roth. SECURE 2.0 requires new plans (established after 29 Dec 2022, effective plan years from 2025) to auto-enroll at 3–10%, but final regulations were still pending as of Oct 2026. The 10% early-withdrawal penalty has many exceptions, and some apply only to IRAs or only to plans.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 limits (401(k), 403(b), governmental 457, TSP) [verified]**: [IRS IR-2025-111](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500)
  - Deferral: $24,500 (2025: $23,500)
  - Catch-up at 50+: **$8,000** (2025: $7,500), total $32,500
  - "Super catch-up" at ages 60–63: **$11,250** (unchanged)
- **Roth catch-up rule for high earners (SECURE 2.0 §603):**
  - If your prior-year FICA wages (W-2 Box 3) from the plan sponsor were over **$150,000**, your catch-up contributions must be Roth. The threshold was $145,000, indexed to $150,000 by Notice 2025-67, so 2025 wages decide 2026 treatment.
  - Self-employment income and spouse income don't count toward the threshold.
  - The mandatory Roth treatment applies from 2026 under good-faith compliance. The final regulations (Sept 2025) take effect 1 Jan 2027.
  - Sources: [PlanSponsor](https://www.plansponsor.com/what-is-the-wage-threshold-requiring-roth-catch-ups-in-2026/), [CAPTRUST](https://www.captrust.com/resources/secure-2-0-roth-catch-up-regulations/), [Vanguard](https://workplace.vanguard.com/insights-and-research/report/from-proposed-to-policy-irs-finalizes-awaited-secure-20-catch-up-contribution-regulations.html), [Gusto](https://support.gusto.com/article/260515165749823/understand-2026-roth-catch-up-for-high-earners). These are secondary; the IRS final regs and Notice 2025-67 were not fetched directly.
- **Auto-enrollment (SECURE 2.0 §101 / IRC 414A):**
  - New 401(k)/403(b) plans must auto-enroll at **3% to 10%**, rising 1% a year to at least 10% and at most 15%. Employees can opt out.
  - Exempt: plans that existed before enactment, employers with 10 or fewer employees, businesses under 3 years old, and church and governmental plans.
  - Sources: [Schneider Downs](https://schneiderdowns.com/our-thoughts-on/secure-2-act-section-101-summary/), [Withum](https://www.withum.com/resources/what-plan-sponsors-should-know-about-section-101-of-secure-act-2-0/).
  - Regulatory status: OIRA finished reviewing the draft final regulations on 16 Oct 2026, and publication was not confirmed: [NAPA, Oct 2026](https://www.napa-net.org/news/2026/10/secure-2.0-automatic-enrollment-regs-clear-white-house-review).
- **10% additional tax** on distributions before 59½ **[verified]**: [IRS exceptions table](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-exceptions-to-tax-on-early-distributions)
  - Exceptions that apply to both plans and IRAs:
    - Birth or adoption: up to $5,000 per child
    - Emergency personal expense: one per year, up to $1,000
    - Domestic abuse: lesser of $10,000 or 50% of the account
    - Federally declared disaster: up to $22,000
    - Also death, disability, substantially equal periodic payments (SEPP), IRS levy, and medical expenses over 7.5% of AGI
  - Plans only:
    - Separation from service in or after the year you turn 55 (50 for public safety workers)
    - Terminal illness (applies to plans; the IRS table doesn't address IRAs)
  - IRAs only:
    - First home: up to $10,000
    - Qualified higher education expenses
  - SIMPLE IRA withdrawals in the first 2 years of participation carry a 25% additional tax instead of 10%.
  - Governmental 457(b) distributions are generally exempt from the 10% tax.
- **Vesting** **[not re-fetched]**: Your own contributions are always 100% vested. Employer matching contributions can vest on a cliff schedule (up to 3 years) or a graded schedule (2–6 years): [DOL EBSA, "What You Should Know About Your Retirement Plan"](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/what-you-should-know-about-your-retirement-plan).
- **Rollovers** **[not re-fetched]**: A direct trustee-to-trustee rollover avoids the mandatory 20% withholding. With an indirect rollover you have 60 days to redeposit: [IRS Rollovers of Retirement Plan and IRA Distributions](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/rollovers-of-retirement-plan-and-ira-distributions).

### Inferences
- Lesson hook: "The employer match is an instant 50–100% return. Contribute at least enough to get the full match." Also tell learners to check the vesting schedule before changing jobs.
- If a learner was auto-enrolled at 3%, they should check whether 3% captures the full match. Often it doesn't.
- The caller's earlier figures ($24,500 / $8,000 / $11,250 / $7,500) are all confirmed.
- Roth vs traditional is a choice about when you pay tax: pay now (Roth) or pay later (traditional). For low-income young workers, Roth is often favoured. This is a general principle, not a sourced claim.

- The text of IRS Notice 2025-67 and the final §414A auto-enrollment regulations were not read directly.
- The DOL vesting and IRS rollover pages were not fetched this session.

## IRAs: Roth vs traditional, 2026 income limits, Saver's Credit → Saver's Match (2027)

### Takeaway
The 2026 IRA limit is $7,500, with a $1,100 catch-up at 50+. The catch-up is now indexed and was $1,000 in 2025. Roth eligibility phases out at modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) of $153k–$168k for single filers and $242k–$252k for joint filers. The Saver's Credit income caps for 2026 are $40,250 single, $60,375 head of household and $80,500 joint. From tax year 2027 the credit becomes the Saver's Match, a federal deposit of 50% of contributions up to $2,000, so at most $1,000 a year, paid into the retirement account.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 IRA figures [verified]**: [IRS IR-2025-111](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500)
  - Contribution limit: **$7,500**
  - Catch-up at 50+: **$1,100**
  - Roth IRA phase-out:
    - Single or head of household: $153,000–$168,000
    - Married filing jointly: $242,000–$252,000
    - Married filing separately: $0–$10,000
  - Traditional IRA deduction phase-out when covered by a workplace plan:
    - Single: $81,000–$91,000
    - Married filing jointly, contributor covered: $129,000–$149,000
    - Married filing jointly, only the spouse covered: $242,000–$252,000
  - Saver's Credit AGI caps:
    - Married filing jointly: **$80,500**
    - Head of household: **$60,375**
    - Single or married filing separately: **$40,250**
- **Saver's Match (SECURE 2.0 §103)**:
  - Replaces the Saver's Credit from **tax year 2027**, with the first deposits expected in 2028.
  - Pays 50% of contributions up to $2,000, so at most **$1,000** a year, deposited into the IRA or plan.
  - Refundable. Not taxed when received; taxed on withdrawal like pre-tax money.
  - Treasury Notice 2026-48 (Aug 2026) began rulemaking and implements EO 14403, which directs a "TrumpIRA.gov" site listing IRAs that accept the match by 1 Jan 2027.
  - Sources: [401k Specialist](https://401kspecialistmag.com/treasury-irs-begin-rulemaking-for-2027-savers-match-advance-trumpira-gov/), [The College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/85989/treasury-and-irs-detail-1000-savers-match-coming-to-retirement-accounts-in-2027/), [IRS RFI, 2024](https://www.irs.gov/node/138326).
  - Conflict: some 2024 coverage ([PlanAdviser](https://www.planadviser.com/federal-regulators-seek-comments-savers-match-contributions/)) said "up to $2,000". That misreads the $2,000 contribution base, and the match itself is capped at $1,000.

### Inferences
- The Saver's Match is highly relevant to the course audience of young, low-income workers. Lesson: "Saving $2,000 can get you $1,000 free from the government from 2027." Flag that final rules are pending.
- The "TrumpIRA.gov" name is politically charged. Present it neutrally as the official Treasury IRA directory if it launches.

### Gaps
- The Notice 2026-48 text was not read. Final eligibility phase-out ranges for the Saver's Match were not confirmed.
- Credit-rate tiers (50% / 20% / 10%) for the 2026 Saver's Credit were not retrieved. See IRS Form 8880.

## Investing basics: index funds, expense ratios, target-date funds, brokerage accounts, SIPC/FDIC, BrokerCheck, fiduciary vs broker, scams

### Takeaway
SIPC protects up to $500,000 per customer (of which $250,000 cash) if a brokerage firm fails. It does not cover market losses, bad advice or most crypto. FDIC covers bank deposits up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. Investment scams were the costliest fraud type reported to the FTC in 2025, with $7.9B lost out of $15.9B total.

### Cited Findings
- **SIPC [verified]**: [SIPC, What SIPC Protects](https://www.sipc.org/for-investors/what-sipc-protects)
  - Limit: **$500,000**, including a **$250,000** cash limit.
  - Not covered:
    - Market losses
    - Worthless securities
    - Bad advice
    - Commodities, futures and foreign exchange
    - Unregistered investment contracts
    - Crypto and digital assets that aren't securities (stablecoins included)
- **FDIC** **[not re-fetched]**: $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category. Stocks, bonds, mutual funds, crypto and annuities are not covered: [FDIC Deposit Insurance](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance).
- **Fraud data, FTC 2025 [verified]**: [FTC JEC testimony, Mar 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc-testimony-jec-hearing-on-the-rising-scam-economy.pdf), [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-testifies-joint-economic-committee-agencys-efforts-combat-fraud)
  - Total: $15.9B lost across 3M fraud reports (2024: about $12.5B).
  - Investment scams: $7.9B, about half of all losses, with an average loss over $10,000. 2024 was $5.7B.
  - Imposter scams: over 1M reports, $3.5B lost.
  - Scams that started on social media: over $2B.
  - Adults 60+: $1.06B lost to investment scams, up 42%. Figure via [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/practice-management/elder-fraud-losses-hit-3b-as-investment-scams-surge-42-ftc-says/268749).
  - Reported losses understate the true total.
- **Fiduciary vs broker, BrokerCheck, scam types** **[not re-fetched]**. These are long-standing pages to link:
  - Investor.gov "Check Out Your Investment Professional", which searches both IAPD and BrokerCheck: [Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/working-investment-professional)
  - [FINRA BrokerCheck](https://brokercheck.finra.org/)
  - SEC Form CRS and Regulation Best Interest: brokers must act in your "best interest" when they make a recommendation. Registered investment advisers owe an ongoing fiduciary duty. [SEC Form CRS](https://www.investor.gov/CRS)
  - SEC/Investor.gov alerts on relationship-investment ("pig butchering") scams, affinity fraud and crypto fraud: [Investor.gov fraud alerts](https://www.investor.gov/protect-your-investments/fraud)
  - Index funds, expense ratios and target-date funds: [Investor.gov glossary and "Mutual Funds and ETFs" guide](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/mutual-funds-and-exchange-traded-1)

### Inferences
- The scam lesson should lead with "investment scams are now the #1 loss category." Pig butchering often starts on social media or with a wrong-number text and moves to fake crypto platforms. That pattern is consistent with the FTC social-media figure, but the link itself is not separately sourced here.
- A compact "safety stack" lesson: FDIC (bank), SIPC (broker failure, not losses), BrokerCheck/IAPD (the person), Form CRS (how they're paid).

### Gaps
- Investor.gov, FDIC and SEC pages were not fetched this session, so none of their 2026-specific statistics are captured.
- No 2026 data was found on average index-fund expense ratios (the ICI publishes these annually; ICI is a trade body).

## Survey data on US retirement readiness

### Takeaway
Only 35% of non-retired US adults think their retirement savings are on track. That is flat since 2024, down from 40% in 2021, and just 7% for incomes under $25k (Fed SHED, May 2026). Worker confidence in having enough for retirement fell to 61%, a 9-year low (EBRI RCS, April 2026).

### Cited Findings
- **Fed SHED** ("Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025", published May 2026): [Federal Reserve SHED](https://www.federalreserve.gov/consumerscommunities/shed.htm), figures via [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/practice-management/fed-survey-reveals-cracks-beneath-stable-surface-of-us-financial-health/266580) and [roic.ai](https://www.roic.ai/news/fed-survey-financial-wellbeing-stable-but-economic-worries-persist-05-13-2026)
  - Sample: about 13,000 adults, surveyed Oct 2025.
  - **35%** of non-retirees say their retirement savings are on track (2024: 35%, 2021: 40%).
  - By income: **7%** under $25k, 59% at $100k+.
  - 73% of adults are doing at least okay financially.
- **EBRI/Greenwald 2026 Retirement Confidence Survey** (released 21 Apr 2026): [EBRI press release](https://www.ebri.org/content/2026-retirement-confidence-survey-finds-americans-less-confident-about-retirement-as-worries-grow-over-social-security--medicare-and-rising-costs), [PlanAdviser](https://www.planadviser.com/worker-retiree-retirement-confidence-hits-lowest-point-in-nearly-a-decade/)
  - Sample: 2,544 adults aged 25+, surveyed Jan 2026 (1,007 workers, 1,045 retirees, 492 caregiver oversample).
  - **61%** of workers are confident of living comfortably in retirement, down from 67% and the lowest since 2017.
  - Confidence in Social Security and Medicare: about 50% among workers, low 60s among retirees (retirees down 5 and 8 points).
  - 88% of workers offered a plan are satisfied with it.
  - Median retirement age among retirees is **62**, and nearly half left work earlier than planned.
- The Fed's Survey of Consumer Finances (a different survey) shows rising balances, e.g. ages 55–64 average $670,200 in 2025. Don't conflate it with SHED: [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/americans-retirement-savings-grow-more-families-participate-plans.amp) (secondary).

### Inferences
- "Most people retire earlier than planned" (EBRI) is a strong motivator for starting early. Pair it with SSA's 62-vs-67 reduction.
- Averages from the Survey of Consumer Finances are skewed by the wealthy. Use medians or skip them.

### Gaps
- The SHED PDF itself was not fetched. Figures come from consistent secondary reports of the Fed release.
- Full RCS data is restricted to EBRI Partners, so only the press-release figures are public.

## Reuse / licence terms (cross-cutting)

### Takeaway
US federal works (SSA, IRS, SEC/Investor.gov, DOL, CFPB, FDIC, FTC, the Federal Reserve Board, CRS) are public domain under 17 U.S.C. §105. FINRA, SIPC, EBRI and trade press are copyrighted. FINRA explicitly forbids reproduction and AI/ML use without written permission, so link to it and don't copy its text.

### Cited Findings
- FINRA terms of use: [FINRA Terms of Use](https://www.finra.org/terms-of-use) **[verified]**
  - Content may be used "ONLY for your own non-commercial personal or professional use."
  - Copying or distribution requires written permission or fair use with FINRA's notices kept.
  - Bulk copying and scraping are prohibited, and so is use with "machine learning, neural network, deep learning" programs.
  - Linking is allowed, but the page must display in full and not inside a frame.
- US government works are not copyrightable: [17 U.S.C. §105](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/105) **[not re-fetched]**.
  - Caveat: some agency pages embed third-party images or logos, and agency seals and logos have separate restrictions.
- EBRI: full RCS findings are reserved for EBRI Partners. See the [EBRI RCS page](https://ebri.org/retirement/retirement-confidence-survey).

### Inferences
- The FINRA AI clause matters directly for an AI-tutored course. Don't ingest FINRA pages into the tutor's corpus. Link to BrokerCheck and Investor.gov (SEC, public domain) instead.
- Quote EBRI and SIPC figures briefly with attribution (fair use) and link to them.

### Gaps
- SIPC's and EBRI's specific terms-of-use pages were not fetched.
