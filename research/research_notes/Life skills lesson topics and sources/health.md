# Health: practical knowledge adults wish school had taught (SA first, then UK, US)

Researched 2026-11-04. Pages fetched live where noted; review/modified dates are quoted from page metadata. Framing for the course: these are facts to teach awareness and when to get help, not medical advice. Every lesson should end with "where to get help" for SA, UK and US.

## Prioritised list of candidate lessons (synthesis — see sections below for sources)

1. **Emergency numbers and when to call them** (SA/UK/US): highest value, cheapest to teach, numbers verified below.
2. **Vital signs: what's normal** (temperature/fever by age, pulse, breathing, SpO2, blood pressure), including where SA/UK/US BP cut-offs differ.
3. **Fever in babies and children: red flags** (NHS has a clean, citable list).
4. **CPR, choking, bleeding, burns, recovery position, stroke (FAST), heart attack**: high value, but key facts were NOT verified in this session (see Gaps).
5. **Medicines safely**: paracetamol limits, hidden paracetamol in cold/flu remedies, antibiotics.
6. **Mental health and crisis lines**: SADAG verified; Samaritans/988 need verification.
7. **Sexual health, HIV/TB testing and PrEP (SA-specific: free at public clinics, lenacapavir rollout 2026).**
8. **Navigating the health system** (SA public clinic vs medical aid; NHS 111/GP/A&E; US insurance): only the NHS part sourced here.
9. Dental care, sleep, vaccinations: not researched in this session (Gaps).

## Vital signs basics

### Takeaway
Adults: fever is 38C or above; resting pulse 60–100 bpm; breathing ~12–18 (some sources say 12–20) per minute; SpO2 95–100%. Blood pressure is where countries differ most: the UK calls 140/90 (clinic) "high", while the US AHA calls 130/80 "stage 1 hypertension".

### Cited Findings
- Adult high temperature "is usually considered to be 38C or above"; get an urgent GP appointment or NHS 111 if you've treated it at home and it isn't improving or is getting worse. Page last reviewed 19 Oct 2026. — [NHS: High temperature (fever) in adults](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/fever-in-adults/)
- Children: fever is 38C or more. Call 111 if a baby is under 3 months with 38C+, or 3–6 months with 39C+ (or you think they have a high temperature); also for rash plus fever, fever lasting 5+ days, not eating/not themselves, or dehydration signs (dry nappies, sunken eyes, no tears). — [NHS: High temperature (fever) in children](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/fever-in-children/)
- Child fever 999 red flags: stiff neck; rash that does not fade under a pressed glass; bothered by light; first febrile fit; unusually cold hands/feet; blue, grey, pale or blotchy skin/lips/tongue ("may be harder to see on brown or black skin"); drowsy and hard to wake; extreme agitation or confusion; breathing difficulty (grunting, stomach sucking under ribs, very fast breathing); not responding normally. — [NHS: fever in children](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/fever-in-children/)
- Measurement: NHS advises a digital thermometer placed inside the top of the armpit for children. (Page reviewed 3 Jan 2024; modified 9 Sep 2026.) — [NHS: fever in children](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/fever-in-children/)
- Adult resting pulse 60–100 bpm; breathing 12–18 breaths/min. — [MedlinePlus: Vital signs](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002341.htm)
- Normal adult resting respiratory rate 12–20/min — [American Lung Association](https://www.lung.org/blog/respiratory-rate-vital-signs) (differs from MedlinePlus's 12–18; teach "roughly 12–20").
- Normal oxygen saturation is 95–100%. — [MedlinePlus: Pulse oximetry](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/pulse-oximetry/)
- UK: high blood pressure is 140/90 or higher when checked by a professional, or 135/85 or higher at home. Get checked if aged 40+ and not checked in 5 years, or if you think you are at risk. Page reviewed 19 Jul 2024, next review 19 Jul 2027. — [NHS: High blood pressure](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/high-blood-pressure/)
- US (AHA): Normal <120 and <80; Elevated 120–129 and <80; Stage 1 hypertension 130–139 or 80–89; Stage 2 140+ or 90+; Severe/hypertensive crisis >180 and/or >120. If >180/120: wait 1 minute, re-measure; if still high without symptoms call your healthcare professional; with chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness, weakness, vision change or difficulty speaking, call 911. — [AHA: Understanding blood pressure readings](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings)

### Inferences
- The same reading (e.g. 134/84) is "normal-ish/not high" in UK terms but "stage 1 hypertension" in US terms. The lesson should teach "what your country's doctors use" and avoid a single universal number.
- Teach temperature in both C and F for the US version (AHA/CDC audiences use F).

### Gaps
- How measurement site changes temperature (oral vs armpit vs ear vs rectal offsets): no authoritative source fetched. MedlinePlus "Body temperature norms" is the likely source to check.
- SA-specific BP thresholds (SA Hypertension Society / national guideline): not found this session.
- Pulse oximeter accuracy on darker skin (FDA has a known safety communication): not verified this session, but it matters for an SA audience.
- Normal pulse/breathing ranges for children/babies: not gathered.
- NHS normal/ideal BP range (90/60–120/80) is on a different NHS page and was not fetched.

## When to see a doctor vs emergency; emergency numbers

### Takeaway
SA: 10111 police, 10177 ambulance, 112 from any mobile (all verified on a provincial government page). UK: 999 for emergencies, 111 when unsure. US: 911. Crisis line 988 (US) is still to be verified.

### Cited Findings
- SA emergency numbers: Police Flying Squad 10111; Ambulance 10177; cellphone emergency 112 (MTN, Vodacom, Cell C and Telkom); Poisons Information Helpline (Western Cape) 0861 555 777. — [Western Cape Government: Know who you can call in an emergency](https://www.westerncape.gov.za/know-who-you-can-call-emergency)
- National government (GovernmentZA social post) also lists SAPS 10111, Ambulance/Fire 10177, cellphone 112, and the GBV Command Centre 0800 428 428. — [GovernmentZA Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/GovernmentZA/posts/-save-these-emergency-numbers-share-this-and-help-save-a-lifegovzaupdates/1184733833692415/) (social media; official account but secondary format)
- Cape Town also lists 107 from a landline for city emergencies (local, not national). — [Cape Town Tourism](https://www.capetown.travel/travelwise/emergency-contacts/)
- UK: go to A&E for "serious injuries and life-threatening emergencies"; use NHS 111 if you're not sure whether you need A&E. — [NHS: When to go to A&E](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-go-to-ae/)
- US: AHA instructs calling 911 for a hypertensive emergency. — [AHA](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings)

### Inferences
- In SA, 10177 is a public ambulance number; private ambulance services (and medical-aid emergency numbers) exist alongside it. A lesson should tell learners to save the number on their medical-aid card if they have one (inference; not sourced).
- 112 is the easiest number to teach for mobile users in SA (and it also works in the UK/EU), but routing quality varies by area (not sourced, check).

### Gaps
- UK 999/112 and US 988 were not fetched from primary pages this session (988lifeline.org and nhs.uk "call 999" pages should be cited before publishing).
- No SA National DoH page found confirming the numbers; the Western Cape provincial page was the best official source.
- No authoritative SA "clinic vs casualty vs call 10177" decision guide found.

## Basic first aid: CPR, choking, bleeding, burns, recovery position, stroke, heart attack

### Takeaway
There is strong evidence for the need (the public says CPR matters but doesn't feel ready), but the step-by-step facts were not verified in this session and must be sourced from resuscitation-council pages before writing lessons.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 American Red Cross survey (500 US adults, nationally representative, published 29 May 2026): 80% say CPR/AED training is important; only 25% feel confident they could act; 50% wrongly think formal training is required to use an AED. AEDs give voice instructions, analyse the rhythm themselves and will not shock unless one is needed. Over 300,000 out-of-hospital cardiac arrests occur in the US each year. — [American Red Cross, 2026](https://www.redcross.org/about-us/news-and-events/news/2026/we-know-cpr-saves-lives-so-why-don-t-we-do-it.html)
- High-school CPR mandates: a PMC study found that although many knew the basics of CPR, they did not know the first steps when encountering a cardiac arrest. — [PMC11949851](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11949851/)

### Inferences
- "You don't need training to use an AED" and "call first, put the phone on speaker" are high-value myth-busters for the lesson.

### Gaps
- Compression rate/depth, hands-only CPR guidance, choking (back blows/abdominal thrusts), bleeding, burns (cool running water duration), recovery position, FAST stroke signs and heart attack signs: none verified. The Resuscitation Council UK URL tried (`/public-resource/cpr`) returned 404. Sources to fetch: resus.org.uk (current URL), Resuscitation Council of Southern Africa (resuscitationcouncil.co.za), AHA/ERC 2025 guidelines, NHS first-aid pages, British Red Cross first-aid pages, SA Red Cross.
- Licence for AHA, Red Cross and Resus Council content: likely all-rights-reserved (link, do not copy). Not confirmed.

## Sexual/reproductive health, HIV/TB testing, vaccinations, medicines, dental, sleep, health systems

### Takeaway
Medicines: the paracetamol rule ("max 8 x 500mg in 24h, 4 hours apart, watch combination products") is verified. SA: HIV and TB tests are free at public clinics, and injectable lenacapavir PrEP began rolling out in public facilities in June 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Paracetamol (adults, 500mg): 1–2 tablets per dose; no more than 8 in 24 hours; at least 4 hours between doses; people under 50kg may need a lower dose; do not combine with other paracetamol-containing products such as cold and flu remedies; contact NHS 111 if you took more than the packet says, because it can damage the liver. (Modified 27 Aug 2026.) — [NHS: Paracetamol for adults](https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/paracetamol-for-adults/)
- SA National DoH: "Visit any public clinic for a FREE and confidential TB test." — [HealthZA Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/HealthZA/posts/endtbsa-tb-doesnt-discriminate-and-neither-does-the-cure-whether-you-are-hiv-pos/1455500736622995/) (official account, social format)
- Lenacapavir (twice-yearly injectable PrEP) in SA: between 8 June and 24 July 2026, 2,339 injections were given across 22 Western Cape public facilities, and nearly 9 in 10 recipients were first-time PrEP users. — [Western Cape Government](https://www.westerncape.gov.za/health-wellness/article/nearly-9-10-people-choosing-lenacapavir-western-cape-are-first-time-users-long-acting-hiv)
- SA lenacapavir implementation guidelines were signed in Dec 2025; an HIV test is required before starting. — [SA Lenacapavir Implementation Guidelines (PrEPWatch PDF)](https://www.prepwatch.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/South-Africa-Lenacapavir-Guidelines-Dec-2025-Signed.pdf); [COSATU repost of DoH notice](https://www.facebook.com/CosatuHeadquartersBraam/posts/the-department-of-health-urges-citizens-to-go-to-their-nearest-participating-fac/982544274775706/) (says national PrEP drive June 2026 to March 2027; social, verify)
- WHO recommends long-acting injectable lenacapavir as an additional HIV prevention choice. — [WHO guideline](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240111608)

### Inferences
- The SA HIV lesson can be strongly practical: free test at any clinic, self-test kits, PrEP options (daily oral or six-monthly injection at participating facilities).

### Gaps
- Antibiotics (finish course / don't use for viral infections / resistance), contraception options, vaccination schedules (SA EPI, NHS, CDC), dental care, sleep: not researched this session.
- SA health system navigation (free primary care at public clinics, medical aid vs gap cover, Council for Medical Schemes PMBs), NHS registration with a GP, and US insurance basics (deductible, copay, in-network): not researched.
- No health.gov.za page fetched directly; official SA info found was via provincial sites and DoH social posts.

## Licence and reuse terms for sources

### Takeaway
CDC content is public domain (copyable); NHS content is OGL v3 with strict attribution and freshness rules (copyable); WHO is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO (OK for a free non-commercial course, and adaptations must be share-alike). Treat AHA, Red Cross, SADAG, Cleveland Clinic and Resus Council pages as link-only.

### Cited Findings
- NHS website content is released under the Open Government Licence v3.0 and can be copied, adapted and used for any purpose, including commercial. Excluded: logos, images (many are stock-licensed), personal data, third-party content, and medical devices. — [NHS terms and conditions](https://www.nhs.uk/our-policies/terms-and-conditions/) (modified 26 Mar 2025)
- NHS attribution: unchanged copies must either show the copy date or be refreshed at least every 7 days, be attributed "Information from the NHS website" (plus "as at DDMMYY" if not refreshed) with a link to the page, and the site must state "Information from the NHS website is licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0". Adapted or non-refreshed content must instead say "Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0" and must NOT cite the NHS as the source. — [NHS terms and conditions](https://www.nhs.uk/our-policies/terms-and-conditions/)
- OGL v3: free to copy, publish, distribute and adapt. — [National Archives OGL v3](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/)
- CDC: "Most of the information on the CDC and ATSDR websites is not subject to copyright, is in the public domain, and may be freely used or reproduced"; CDC content should not be framed inside its original window. — [CDC: Use of agency materials](https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html)
- WHO: materials under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO can be copied, translated and adapted for non-commercial use with WHO acknowledged; other WHO material needs permission. Adaptations are expected to use a similar licence (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 or 4.0). — [WHO copyright](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/copyright)
- CDC pages were removed and later ordered restored in 2025, so availability has been unstable. — [CIDRAP](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/public-health/removal-pages-cdc-website-brings-confusion-dismay); [Infectious Disease Advisor](https://www.infectiousdiseaseadvisor.com/news/federal-judge-rules-fda-cdc-must-restore-health-websites-removed-under-trump-order/)

### Inferences
- Practical policy for the course: write original lesson text and cite NHS/CDC/WHO, rather than copying NHS text. Copying triggers the 7-day refresh or date-stamp rule, while adapting means you can't name the NHS as the source.
- WHO's NC-SA terms would force the course's own adapted material to be NC-SA. That is fine if the repos are meant to be CC BY-NC-SA, but it is a problem if the course licence is more permissive.
- MedlinePlus: NLM-authored pages are generally public domain, but the A.D.A.M. encyclopedia pages (`/ency/`) are licensed content. Verify before copying (not sourced this session).

### Gaps
- Licence terms for SA DoH, NICD, SADAG, SA Red Cross and the Resuscitation Council of SA: not found; assume all rights reserved and link only.
- MedlinePlus reuse terms not fetched.
