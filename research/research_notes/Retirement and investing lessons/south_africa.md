# South Africa: Retirement and Investing Basics (for the "Things They Don't Teach You" beginner course)

Researched 5 November 2026, for the 2027 tax year (1 March 2026 – 28 February 2027). Some facts were already settled before this work and are not repeated here: the TFSA limits (R46,000 a year, R500,000 lifetime), the two-pot withdrawal minimum (R2,000, once per tax year, taxed at your marginal rate) and the retirement-contribution deduction cap of R430,000.

SARS, gov.za (© 2026 Government of South Africa, with a separate "Terms and conditions" link I did not read), FSCA myMoney ("Copyright © FSCA Consumer Education") and rsaretailbonds.gov.za ("All rights reserved © 2019") are all treated as **link and paraphrase only**. ## Retirement system: fund types, two-pot, changing jobs, tax tables, Reg 28, retiring, GEPF

### Takeaway
Since 1 September 2024 every retirement fund (pension, provident or RA) splits money into a vested pot (old money, old rules), a savings pot (one-third of new contributions, one withdrawal per tax year) and a retirement pot (two-thirds, locked until retirement and used to buy a pension). Cashing out the vested pot when you change jobs is taxed on its own lifetime table, which has only R27,500 tax-free. Lump sums taken at retirement are far cheaper: the first R550,000 is tax-free. SARS says both tables are unchanged for 2026 and 2027.

### Cited Findings
**Two-pot structure (FSCA)**
- The two-pot system started on 1 September 2024 and creates three pots. The **Vested Pot** holds everything saved before 1 Sept 2024 plus growth, takes no new contributions and keeps the old rules. The **Savings Pot** holds seed capital, one-third of contributions from 1 Sept 2024 and growth. The **Retirement Pot** holds two-thirds of contributions from 1 Sept 2024. — [FSCA myMoney two-pot FAQ](https://www.fscamymoney.co.za/Pages/two-pot-system-retirement-system-FAQ.aspx)
- **Seed capital:** 10% of the Vested Pot, capped at R30,000, was moved into the Savings Pot. — [FSCA myMoney](https://www.fscamymoney.co.za/Pages/two-pot-system-retirement-system-FAQ.aspx)
- Savings-pot withdrawals also incur fund admin fees, and any SARS debt is deducted first. — [FSCA myMoney](https://www.fscamymoney.co.za/Pages/two-pot-system-retirement-system-FAQ.aspx); [SARS media release, 31 Jan 2025](https://www.sars.gov.za/media-release/more-than-2-million-taxpayers-withdraw-from-their-savings-pot/)
- **Resignation or retrenchment:** you can still take the Vested Pot under the old rules. The Retirement Pot can only be used to buy an annuity at retirement. — [FSCA myMoney](https://www.fscamymoney.co.za/Pages/two-pot-system-retirement-system-FAQ.aspx)
- **At retirement:** you may take one-third of the Vested Pot in cash, and the other two-thirds must buy an annuity. The whole Retirement Pot must buy an annuity. **De minimis rule:** if two-thirds of the Vested Pot plus the Retirement Pot comes to R165,000 or less, you may take the whole amount as cash. — [FSCA myMoney](https://www.fscamymoney.co.za/Pages/two-pot-system-retirement-system-FAQ.aspx)
- When you move to another employer's fund, all three pots transfer. Housing loans are capped at 65% of all three pots combined. Retirement savings are protected from creditors until they are withdrawn. — [FSCA myMoney](https://www.fscamymoney.co.za/Pages/two-pot-system-retirement-system-FAQ.aspx)
- Excluded from two-pot: unclaimed-benefit funds, pensioners, beneficiary funds and closed or dormant funds. Legacy RAs may be exempted, but the conditions were still "under consultation" on that page. — [FSCA myMoney](https://www.fscamymoney.co.za/Pages/two-pot-system-retirement-system-FAQ.aspx)
- FSCA consumer helpline: 0800 20 3722; email CED.Consumer@fsca.co.za. — [FSCA myMoney](https://www.fscamymoney.co.za/Pages/two-pot-system-retirement-system-FAQ.aspx)
- Further official FAQs: [National Treasury two-pot FAQ (Aug 2024, PDF)](https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2024/2024%20Two-pot%20System%20Updated%20%20FAQ%20August%202024.pdf); [Pension Funds Adjudicator FAQ (PDF)](https://pfa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/FAQ%20-%20Relating%20to%20the%20Two-Pot%20System.pdf)

**Lump-sum tax tables (SARS: "no changes" for the 2026 and 2027 tax years, so the 2025 tables still apply)**
- **Withdrawal benefits** (cashing out before retirement, e.g. the vested pot when you resign) — [SARS retirement lump sum benefits](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-rates/income-tax/retirement-lump-sum-benefits/):
  - R1 – R27,500: 0%
  - R27,501 – R726,000: 18% of the amount above R27,500
  - R726,001 – R1,089,000: R125,730 + 27% of the amount above R726,000
  - R1,089,001 and above: R223,740 + 36% of the amount above R1,089,000
- **Retirement lump sums and severance benefits:**
  - R1 – R550,000: 0%
  - R550,001 – R770,000: 18% of the amount above R550,000
  - R770,001 – R1,155,000: R39,600 + 27% of the amount above R770,000
  - R1,155,001 and above: R143,550 + 36% of the amount above R1,155,000
  — [SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-rates/income-tax/retirement-lump-sum-benefits/)
- **Both tables are cumulative over your whole life.** SARS taxes each new lump sum on the running total, which includes withdrawal benefits since March 2009, retirement lump sums since October 2007 and severance benefits since March 2011. Cashing out early therefore uses up the tax-free amount you would otherwise get at retirement. — [SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-rates/income-tax/retirement-lump-sum-benefits/)
- Two-pot savings withdrawals are **not** taxed on these tables. They are added to income and taxed at the marginal rate (18%–45%). — [SARS media release](https://www.sars.gov.za/media-release/more-than-2-million-taxpayers-withdraw-from-their-savings-pot/)
- **2027 rebates:** primary R17,820, secondary (65+) R9,765, tertiary (75+) R3,249. **2027 tax thresholds:** under 65 R99,000; 65+ R153,250; 75+ R171,300. — [SARS rates of tax for individuals](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-rates/income-tax/rates-of-tax-for-individuals/)

**Regulation 28 and annuities**
- Under Regulation 28, retirement funds may invest up to 75% in equities (shares) and 45% offshore. The offshore limit was raised from 30% in 2022. — [10X](https://www.10x.co.za/blog/living-annuity-planning-balancing-freedom-and-responsibility-outside-regulation-28); [BusinessTech/Old Mutual Wealth](https://businesstech.co.za/news/industry-news/637219/regulation-28-navigating-offshore-exposure-limits/)
- Living annuities are generally **not** bound by Reg 28 (they fall under the Insurance Act), so they can be up to 100% offshore. The exception is a living annuity owned by the retirement fund. — [10X FAQ](https://old.10x.co.za/faq/living-annuity/can-i-invest-100-of-my-living-annuity-offshore); [Daily Investor](https://dailyinvestor.com/investing/56087/warning-for-south-africans-taking-retirement-savings-offshore/)
- **Living annuity drawdown:** you choose 2.5%–17.5% of the capital a year, once a year on the policy anniversary. Contracts that started before 21 February 2007 used 5%–20%. The temporary COVID 0.5%–20% relief has ended. — [10X FAQ](https://www.10x.co.za/faq/withdraw-living-annuity); [ASISA media release](https://www.asisa.org.za/media-releases/living-annuity-drawdown-rate-average-drops-below-6-for-the-first-time-since-2011/)
- The average living-annuity drawdown is reported at about 6.6% for 2025. — [Sunday World (ASISA data)](https://sundayworld.co.za/business/living-annuity-assets-climb-by-r911bn-as-retirees-curb-their-spending/) (a news report, not checked against the ASISA release)

**GEPF (Government Employees Pension Fund)**
- GEPF is not under the Pension Funds Act, so its members **cannot** complain to the Pension Funds Adjudicator. They complain to the Government Employees Pension Ombud (GEPO, gepo.co.za) instead. A complaint needs your full name, ID number, Persal/employment number and GEPF number. GEPO has warned members not to pay agents to lodge complaints. — [City Press/News24](https://www.news24.com/citypress/business/recourse-for-members-as-gepf-establishes-its-own-complaints-handling-office-20210813); [Business Report, Apr 2026](https://businessreport.co.za/business/2026-04-30-government-employees-pension-fund-warns-beneficiaries-against-paying-for-complaint-assistance/)
- Scale: about 1.2 million active members and more than 450,000 pensioners and beneficiaries (2021). Assets are about R2.7 trillion per an undated parliamentary report. — [City Press](https://www.news24.com/citypress/business/recourse-for-members-as-gepf-establishes-its-own-complaints-handling-office-20210813); [PMG](https://pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/43117/)
- GEPF's own two-pot explainer: [gepf.co.za](https://www.gepf.co.za/?p=669)

### Inferences
- **Worked example: changing jobs** (first-ever withdrawal, my own arithmetic from the SARS table). Cashing out a R100,000 vested pot costs 18% × (R100,000 − R27,500) = **R13,050 tax**. It also uses up R100,000 of the R550,000 tax-free retirement amount, because the tables are cumulative. This is the core "preserve, don't cash out" lesson.
- **Worked example: retiring.** A first R1,000,000 retirement lump sum is taxed R39,600 + 27% × (R1,000,000 − R770,000) = **R101,700**. A R550,000 lump sum is tax-free.
- Plain-language fund types for lessons (common knowledge, not re-verified here): pension fund (employer), provident fund (employer, now under the same annuity rules as pension funds), RA (personal, used by the self-employed), preservation fund (receives money when you leave a job). Under two-pot, all of them work the same way for new contributions.
- Since few people retire with large balances, the R165,000 de minimis rule matters to many readers: small pots can be taken fully in cash.

### Gaps
- **Preservation-fund rule:** one full or partial withdrawal is allowed before retirement under the old rules. I did not confirm how two-pot interacts with this, and this is from background knowledge only, so it needs a source.
- **Provident funds:** members aged 55 or older on 1 March 2021 keep full cash access to their old provident money (the 2021 annuitisation transition). This is from background knowledge only; the FSCA page did not cover it.
- **Guaranteed vs living annuity:** I found no official comparison page. The idea is well known (a guaranteed annuity pays for life; with a living annuity the money can run out but you choose investments and can leave the rest to heirs), but it has no source in these notes.
- **2026 Budget:** I did not confirm whether it proposed changes to the lump-sum tables. SARS's page says "no changes" for 2027.

## SASSA Older Person's Grant (2026)

### Takeaway
The grant is up to R2,400 a month at ages 60–74 and R2,420 at 75+, from April 2026. It is means-tested on both income (R86,280 a year single) and assets (R1,227,600 single).

### Cited Findings
- **Amounts:** maximum R2,400/month; R2,420 for people over 75. **Age:** 60+. — [gov.za Older Persons Grant](https://www.gov.za/services/social-benefits-retirement-and-old-age/old-age-pension)
- **Means test:** income limit R86,280/yr single and R172,560/yr married; asset limit R1,227,600 single and R2,455,200 married. — [gov.za](https://www.gov.za/services/social-benefits-retirement-and-old-age/old-age-pension)
- **Applying:** go in person to a SASSA office with a 13-digit barcoded ID, proof of marital status, residence, income, assets and any private pension, plus three months of bank statements (and UIF proof if you were employed). There is no online form. Processing takes up to three months and the grant is backdated to the application date. Applying is free. — [gov.za](https://www.gov.za/services/social-benefits-retirement-and-old-age/old-age-pension)
- The increase took effect in April 2026 after the February 2026 Budget. — [Citizen/Kempton Express, May 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/kempton-express/news-headlines/local-news/2026/05/22/means-test-explained-as-sassa-updates-social-grant-limits-for-2026/)

### Inferences
- Lesson point: R2,400 a month is a safety net, not a retirement plan. Someone with a few modest savings can still qualify, because the asset limit is over R1.2m.

### Gaps
- The gov.za page is undated (it has a "R R 2 400" typo), and I did not reach sassa.gov.za itself. Re-check the amounts before publishing and again after each February budget.

## Investing products: TFSA, unit trusts vs ETFs, fees, JSE, offshore allowance, money market, fixed deposits, retail bonds

### Takeaway
To compare costs, beginners should look at the EAC (effective annual cost), not the TER alone, because EAC adds advice, admin and platform fees. Since April 2026 the offshore allowance is R2m a year without tax clearance (the SDA). RSA Retail Savings Bonds start at R1,000 but current rates could not be checked against the official site.

### Cited Findings
- **TER** is the fund's running cost (management fee plus admin). **TC** is transaction costs. **TIC** (Total Investment Cost) = TER + TC, as shown on fund fact sheets. — [Moneyweb/1Life explainer](https://www.1life.co.za/blog/investment-fees); [Profile's Unit Trusts handbook, Mar 2026](https://www.fundsdata.co.za/ebook/2026-1U/files/basic-html/page59.html)
- **EAC (Effective Annual Cost):** an ASISA standard from 2016. It combines investment management, advice, admin and other fees (including initial and exit charges) into one annual percentage, so products can be compared. It also shows that costs weigh more if you hold for a short time: in one sample product, 1.7% a year over 10 years but 2.6% a year over 3 years. — [ASISA EAC standard via Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/asisa-standard-on-effective-annual-cost/); [FA News](https://www.fanews.co.za/article/compliance-regulatory/2/general/1082/new-effective-annual-cost-standard-from-asisa/21038)
- A unit trust's EAC is quoted as if you buy directly from the manager. Broker commission and platform/wrapper fees (e.g. inside an RA) can push the real cost higher. — [1Life](https://www.1life.co.za/blog/investment-fees)
- **Offshore allowance:** the 2026 Budget doubled the Single Discretionary Allowance (SDA) to **R2 million per adult per calendar year**. No SARS tax-clearance PIN is needed, and it covers travel, gifts and investment. The SARB brought it in around 8–17 April 2026 (sources differ on the date). The **Foreign Investment Allowance** is **R10 million per calendar year** and needs a SARS "AIT" PIN. Each spouse has their own allowances. — [Moonstone: SARB finalises reforms](https://www.moonstone.co.za/sarb-finalises-budget-linked-exchange-control-reforms/); [Allan Gray](https://www.allangray.co.za/latest-insights/offshore-investing/higher-discretionary-allowance-the-opportunity-to-invest-more-offshore/); [Daily Maverick, Apr 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-04-08-you-can-take-more-cash-offshore-but-tax-and-timing-traps-still-lurk)
- **RSA Retail Savings Bonds:** fixed-rate bonds run for 2, 3 or 5 years; inflation-linked bonds for 3, 5 or 10 years. Minimum R1,000. — [gov.za FAQ](https://www.gov.za/faq/money-matters/how-do-i-invest-rsa-retail-savings-bonds)
- Retail bond contacts: helpline (012) 315 5888; walk-in centre at 40 Church Square, Pretoria (booking needed); queries@rsaretailbonds.gov.za; you can apply online. The official site's rates page was "temporarily unavailable" when fetched. — [rsaretailbonds.gov.za](https://www.rsaretailbonds.gov.za/)
- A third-party table (2 June 2026) lists 8.5% fixed for 2/3/5 years and inflation-linked real rates of 2.75%/3%/4%. The page contradicts itself, so these are **not verified**. — [ratecompare](https://ratecompare.pages.dev/faq/rsa-retail-bonds)

### Inferences
- Ranking for lessons, cheapest and simplest first: (1) emergency fund in a money market or notice account; (2) TFSA holding a low-cost index ETF or unit trust; (3) retirement fund or RA for the tax deduction; (4) offshore exposure, mostly through local feeder funds or ETFs, so beginners rarely need the SDA at all.
- Lesson point: a 1 percentage-point difference in EAC compounds into a large share of final savings over 30 years. Use a calculator; the arithmetic is standard.

### Gaps
- I did not research money market funds, fixed deposits, JSE basics or the unit trust vs ETF mechanics (trading on an exchange vs buying from the manager; brokerage costs) from primary sources. Those lessons can rely on general knowledge plus ASISA's or the JSE's investor-education pages, which still need finding and checking for reuse terms.
- **TFSA over-contribution penalty:** 40% of the excess, from background knowledge only. It needs a SARS TFSA page citation.
- Current RSA Retail Bond rates: re-fetch rsaretailbonds.gov.za/rates.

## Protections: FSCA, Pension Funds Adjudicator, FAIS Ombud, checking an FSP, scams

### Takeaway
Before paying anyone, search the FSCA's FSP register by both name **and** number. Scammers quote real FSP numbers that belong to other firms. MTI (2020) is the defining SA case: it promised up to 10% a month and took billions of rand. Complaints are free: the Pension Funds Adjudicator handles retirement funds (in writing only), GEPO handles GEPF, and the FAIS Ombud handles advisers.

### Cited Findings
- **How to check an FSP:** on fsca.co.za, use "Financial Service Providers Search" and enter the FSP name or number. Make sure the name matches the number. Make sure the licence is active, not suspended, withdrawn or lapsed (licences renew yearly). Be wary of any provider that won't give its FSP number. — [justmoney](https://www.justmoney.co.za/articles/what-does-it-mean-to-be-a-registered-financial-services-provider); [Moonstone: FSCA warns check who is behind the FSP name](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fsca-warns-check-who-is-behind-the-fsp-name/)
- **Fake FSP numbers:** e.g. Patex Capital used a fake FSP number, per the FSCA. — [Sunday Independent](https://thesundayindependent.pressreader.com/article/282153589416454)
- **2026 FSCA priorities:** the FSCA's annual report to 31 March 2026 names online harm, unregistered insurance and trading signals. Most warnings were about unlicensed investment or forex/trading offers. Named warnings include Equity Edge (4 Mar 2026), Denco Financial Services (Feb 2026), and Daily Financial Earners (Jun 2026, which copied the branding of licensed firms). There are also impersonators claiming to be FSCA staff. — [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fsca-sharpens-focus-on-online-harm-and-unauthorised-advice/); [IOL, Mar 2026](https://iol.co.za/personal-finance/financial-planning/2026-03-05-fsca-warns-south-africans-about-equity-edge/)
- **MTI (Mirror Trading International):** it promised up to 10% a month from "bitcoin trading", and the FSCA found no evidence of trading success. It was provisionally liquidated in December 2020 after clients couldn't withdraw. Estimates of what it took range from about R2.9bn to R9.45bn. Liquidators are clawing back "profits" paid to earlier investors, with FSCA support. — [Citizen](https://citizen.co.za/business/money-down-the-drain-bitcoin-scheme-mirror-trading-international-to-be-liquidated); [Moonstone/FSCA report](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fsca-reports-on-investigation-into-mirror-trading-international-pty-ltd/); [IOL](https://www.iol.co.za/business-report/companies/liquidators-intend-to-recover-money-investors-gained-before-mtis-demise-82371b9b-0167-4e7f-8edb-970edd25b37f); [TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/anatomy-of-a-bitcoin-scam-how-mti-duped-investors/170283)
- **Pension Funds Adjudicator:** free, no lawyer needed. Complaints must be in writing (none by phone). It covers only funds under the Pension Funds Act, so not GEPF. — [legalfundi](https://legalfundi.com/legal-question/complaints-about-payments-from-pension-funds); [City Press](https://www.news24.com/citypress/business/recourse-for-members-as-gepf-establishes-its-own-complaints-handling-office-20210813); [pfa.org.za](https://pfa.org.za/)
- SARS warns that understating income on two-pot directive applications is tax evasion and carries penalties. — [SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/media-release/more-than-2-million-taxpayers-withdraw-from-their-savings-pot/)

### Inferences
- A scam checklist for a phone lesson: guaranteed or very high monthly returns ("10% a month" = MTI); pressure to recruit others; payment into personal accounts or crypto wallets; an FSP number that doesn't match the name on the FSCA register; contact only through WhatsApp or Telegram. Each item follows from the FSCA warnings and the MTI facts above.
- MTI clawbacks teach a non-obvious lesson: even "winners" in a Ponzi scheme can be forced to pay money back.

### Gaps
- **FAIS Ombud:** I did not fetch its contact details or remit (complaints about advisers and intermediaries, free). Get them from faisombud.co.za.
- The FSCA's own warnings-list URL was not fetched directly. Point lessons to fsca.co.za → "Regulatory Actions / Warnings".
- Phone numbers conflict across sources (FSCA 0800 20 3722 on the myMoney page vs 0800 110 443 in a 2018 article). Use 0800 20 3722, which comes from the FSCA's own page.
- The final MTI court outcome (Ponzi declaration) and recovery status in 2026 are unconfirmed.

## Survey data: SA retirement readiness and two-pot withdrawal statistics

### Takeaway
The figure most often quoted is that only about 6% of South Africans can retire comfortably. It is attributed to National Treasury, but I could not trace it to a primary source. 2026 surveys show more people have plans (FNB: 73% of under-60s) but over half say they are falling short. Two-pot withdrawals reached R43.4bn by 31 January 2025, from 2.4m approved tax directives.

### Cited Findings
- **SARS (31 Jan 2025):** 2,664,279 tax directive applications; 2,403,379 approved; R43.42bn gross paid out; 213,654 taxpayers flagged for declaring incorrect income. — [SARS media release](https://www.sars.gov.za/media-release/more-than-2-million-taxpayers-withdraw-from-their-savings-pot/)
- By about June 2025, cumulative two-pot withdrawals were reported at about R57bn. — [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/r35bn-withdrawn-as-sars-reports-unprecedented-two-pot-system-activity/) (secondary source)
- **Alexforbes, 2027 tax year:** more than 210,000 savings-pot claims between 1 and 27 March 2026. 67% of members who claimed in the prior tax year claimed again. — [Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/business/you-can-draw-from-your-two-pot-savings-again-but-you-probably-shouldnt) (reported via search summary)
- **FNB Retirement Insights Survey 2026:** 73% of South Africans under 60 have a retirement plan, up from 60%. Over half say they are falling short on retirement savings. — [FA News](https://www.fanews.co.za/article/retirement/1357/general/1358/fnb-retirement-insights-survey-more-south-africans-are-planning-for-retirement-but-rising-costs-still-test-readiness/44345)
- **Sanlam Benchmark 2026:** people think planning should start in their mid-30s, but most seek advice only about 20 months before retiring. Sanlam commentary says most can only afford to stop working after 80. — [EWN, 25 Jun 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/06/25/south-africans-still-leaving-retirement-planning-far-too-late-sanlam-survey)
- "Only 6% of South Africans can retire comfortably" is attributed to National Treasury by Old Mutual, a secondary source. — via search results; primary source not found
- Just Retirement Insights 2026 report (PDF): [ebnet](https://www.ebnet.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/just-retirement-insights-2026.pdf) (not read)

### Inferences
- Use repeat withdrawals as the hook for the "savings pot is not an ATM" lesson (two-thirds of claimants withdraw again the next year), along with the tax and lost compounding.

### Gaps
- I found no official SARS or Treasury cumulative two-pot total for 2026. A blog claim of 1.42m claims and R28.4bn for 2026/27 to 30 June is unverified and not used.
- The "6%" figure has no primary Treasury source. Present it as "widely cited" or leave it out.
